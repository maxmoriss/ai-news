### Governance Under Fire: Nvidia's Tumultuous Week
The fallout from Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia continues. S&P Global Ratings is considering a credit downgrade for the chipmaker, citing governance and "key man" risks, potentially lowering its rating to AA- **[S&P considers Nvidia credit downgrade](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/sp-considers-nvidia-credit-downgrade-over-governance-and-key-man-risks/)**. Internally, Huang is facing consequences for breaking the company's own code of conduct, an act employees may not forget **[Jensen Huang broke Nvidia’s code of conduct](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/jensen-huang-broke-nvidias-code-of-conduct-and-his-employees-may-not-forget-it/)**. In an all-hands meeting, Huang thanked the White House for its support and revealed he is in therapy **[Huang thanks White House, says he’s in therapy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/in-all-hands-meeting-jensen-huang-thanks-white-house-and-says-hes-in-therapy/)**. Despite the chaos, Huang maintains his controversial stance that a formal succession plan creates unnecessary hierarchy **[Huang still thinks succession plans create hierarchy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/jensen-huang-still-thinks-a-succession-plan-creates-unnecessary-hierarchy/)**.

The scrutiny extends beyond internal culture. Investigators still have questions about what Nvidia knew regarding the end-use of chips sold to partner Megaspeed **[Questions remain about Megaspeed](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/the-questions-jensen-huang-and-nvidia-will-still-have-to-answer-about-megaspeed/)**. A Yale professor has gone further, arguing that Nvidia has been a "detriment" to U.S. industrial might by helping put advanced chips in adversaries' hands **[Yale’s Sonnenfeld: Nvidia is a ‘detriment’](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/yales-sonnenfeld-nvidia-is-a-detriment-to-u-s-industrial-might-not-a-champion/)**.

### Policy, Privacy, and Practical Limits
Regulatory and ethical concerns are mounting across the AI landscape. In a significant consumer protection move, **New York has banned AI chatbot toys for five years** following research revealing safety failures, including exposure to inappropriate content **[NY bans AI toys](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/want-to-buy-your-kid-an-ai-toy-this-christmas-not-in-new-york/)**. Meanwhile, state Attorneys General are challenging Character.AI's bankruptcy plan, which proposes auctioning off private chat data from over 21 million users, including 2.1 million minors **[AGs fight Character.AI chat auction](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/)**. Cloud backup service Backblaze also found itself clarifying its data policies, telling Ars Technica it shares customer *information* with advertisers, but not their actual backup files **[Backblaze explains data sharing](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/11/backblaze-explains-why-its-sharing-customer-information-with-advertisers/)**.

On the product side, **OpenAI announced that heavy users may hit usage limits sooner** as it merges its ChatGPT and ChatGPT Work experiences into a single platform with one unified usage meter **[OpenAI merges Chat and Work](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/openai-says-heavy-chatgpt-users-may-hit-limits-sooner-as-it-merges-chat-and-work/)**. And a new benchmark highlights a core challenge for AI agents: even the best models **struggle with completing everyday tasks** reliably and safely **[AI agents struggle with everyday life](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/even-the-best-ai-agents-struggle-with-everyday-life/)**.

### Infrastructure, Agents, and Odd Patents
The race for AI infrastructure is hitting new frontiers and new hurdles. The **FCC authorized SpaceX** to deploy up to 1 million AI chip-equipped satellites for its "Starmind" space-based data center initiative **[FCC signs off on SpaceX data center satellites](https://www.theverge.com/science/1042278/spacex-fcc-starmind-ai-space-data-centers)**. Back on Earth, building AI data centers is becoming harder, with Nvidia refusing to backstop projects until they are nearly complete, squeezing "neocloud" startups **[AI data centers harder to build](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/ai-data-centers-are-becoming-harder-to-build-and-neoclouds-are-feeling-it/)**.

In the agent ecosystem, **Amazon and Meta are in talks** to lift Amazon's blockade on the Muse AI shopping agent, with discussions centering on product recommendations and ad revenue **[Amazon, Meta in Muse talks](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/amazon-and-meta-reportedly-in-talks-to-lift-blockade-on-ai-agent-muse/)**. Walmart, however, is already onboard, teaming up with Meta to bring Black Friday deals to Muse **[Walmart teams up with Meta for Muse deals](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/walmart-teams-up-with-meta-to-bring-black-friday-deals-to-ai-agent-muse/)**.

Elsewhere, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei pushed back against accusations that he favors surrendering human control to AI, calling the claims "absurd" **[Amodei calls accusations ‘absurd’](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/anthropics-dario-amodei-calls-accusations-of-surrendering-human-control-to-ai-absurd/)**. Nothing offered a full look at its upcoming AI earbuds, the Ear Note, which can record and summarize conversations **[Nothing reveals AI earbuds](https://www.theverge.com/gadgets/1042162/nothing-ear-note-ai-earbuds-reveal)**. Google had a patent rejected for "My Channel," a system to create feeds of personalized, AI-generated videos and ads—though it may appeal **[Google’s bleak AI video patent](https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1042221/google-my-channel-ai-video-patent)**. Finally, the rise of "vibe-coding" AI agents is causing chaos in the music synth industry, forcing companies to confront open-source firmware modding **[Vibe coding chaos in music gear](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1040081/vibe-coding-open-source-music-firmware)**.

**Editorial Take:** Today's news paints a picture of an industry hitting its adolescent phase: phenomenal power coupled with glaring growing pains. The intense focus on Nvidia's governance shows that market dominance alone isn't enough; institutional maturity and ethical accountability are now critical to maintaining trust—from rating agencies, employees, and the public. Simultaneously, the policy actions in New York and against Character.AI signal that regulators are moving from theoretical concern to concrete enforcement, particularly where children and consumer data are involved. The theme of the day is **accountability**. As AI becomes more woven into daily life and global infrastructure, the companies building it are being held to a higher standard, whether they're ready or not.
