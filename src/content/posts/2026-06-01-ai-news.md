### **Startups & Products: New Models, New Defenses**
The race for superior AI models isn't just about chatbots. **Windborne Systems' newest weather forecasting model is reportedly beating the best government predictions by days,** showcasing the potential for specialized AI to disrupt established scientific fields. [Read more on TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/this-ai-weather-startup-is-out-forecasting-government-agencies/).

Meanwhile, a counter-trend is gaining steam. As AI becomes ubiquitous in search, **DuckDuckGo is launching 'no AI' web extensions for Chrome and Firefox,** making its traditional, algorithm-based search engine easier to access amidst a reported traffic boom. [Read more on TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/duckduckgo-makes-its-no-ai-search-engine-easier-to-access-as-its-traffic-booms/).

On the platform side, **Microsoft is poised to unveil new AI models and Windows improvements at its Build developer conference** this week, a critical moment as the company continues its full-scale pivot to an AI-centric business. [Read more on The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/report/940861/microsoft-build-ai-models-windows-dev-mode-what-to-expect).

### **Policy & Ethics: Scraping, Secrecy, and Side Effects**
The economic and environmental externalities of the AI boom are forcing reactions. The fitness app **Strava is restricting and monetizing access to its API, blaming a surge in "zero-code AI apps and scrapers"** for data. Developers will now need a paid subscription. [Read more on The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/gadgets/940854/strava-restricts-api-access-ai-apps).

The infrastructure powering AI is also facing scrutiny. **Environmental activist Erin Brockovich has taken aim at data center secrecy,** launching a new campaign focused on the local impacts of these energy- and water-intensive facilities. [Read more on TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/31/erin-brockovich-takes-aim-at-data-center-secrecy/).

A more philosophical debate is also brewing. A new podcast episode is **trying to make sense of the concept of "AI psychosis,"** questioning whether tech leaders are uniquely susceptible to irrational beliefs about artificial intelligence's capabilities. [Read more on TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/31/making-sense-of-the-debate-over-ai-psychosis/).

### **AI in the Wild: From Games to Grammys**
AI's creative and disruptive force is being felt across industries. In gaming, **a survey indicates 90% of developers are already integrating AI into their daily work,** with a massive year-over-year increase in Steam games disclosing AI use. It's restructuring development pipelines. [Read more on AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/ai-in-video-game-development/).

This upheaval reaches the highest levels of creative recognition. The CEO of the Recording Academy, **Harvey Mason Jr., discusses how the Grammys should handle AI's explosion in music,** two years after initial warnings about the technology's impact. [Read more on The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/940831/ai-grammys-music-recording-harvey-mason).

Finally, in a bizarre fusion of trends, one reporter went on a quest to find **an AI-powered weed vape that purportedly rewards users with Bitcoin,** a story that highlights the strange and often grifty frontiers of AI marketing. [Read more on The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/933916/ai-powered-crypto-cannabis-vape).

*(Editorial Note: Today's stories paint a picture of AI at an inflection point. Its technical prowess is undeniable, beating experts at their own game. Yet, the backlash—in the form of "no-AI" products, API crackdowns, environmental activism, and cultural anxiety—is equally real. The narrative is no longer just about building smarter models, but about managing their very real-world consequences.)*
