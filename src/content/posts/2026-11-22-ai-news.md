### The Physical Backlash & The Digital Frontier
The unseen engines of the AI boom are facing new scrutiny. A new book, **"Hyperscale" by Paris Marx**, argues that the massive, resource-intensive data centers powering Big Tech and AI are becoming focal points for public and political backlash, driven by their environmental impact and community disruptions ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/22/why-data-centers-are-at-the-center-of-the-big-tech-backlash/)). This comes as **Nvidia** presents a bifurcated image: soaring earnings fueled by AI hardware demand exist alongside calls from some tech executives for a strategic rethink ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/a-tale-of-two-nvidias/)).

Meanwhile, AI is embedding itself deeper into our personal digital spaces. A new wave of **AI agents designed to live in your text messages** is emerging, offering help with everything from general tasks to family coordination and travel planning ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/all-the-ai-agents-that-can-live-in-your-text-messages/)). This push towards ambient, vibe-based computing is extending to gadgets, with "vibe-coding" promising to let users control TVs, cameras, and more through simple natural language commands ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/column/1039139/vibe-coding-gadgets-tv-camera)).

### Content, Creativity, and Control
The ethical and practical dilemmas of generative AI are playing out across creative industries. In gaming, **The Binding of Isaac: Repentance Plus** is being review-bombed on Steam after players discovered a segment used AI-generated music from Suno, leading the developer to promptly remove the tracks ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1039770/binding-of-isaac-ai-music)). In entertainment, a new trend sees actors licensing AI versions of their faces to "microdrama" startups, a practice already popular in China, raising questions about the future of acting and digital likeness rights ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/is-selling-an-ai-version-of-your-face-to-this-microdrama-startup-the-future-of-hollywood/)).

Privacy concerns are driving product changes, too. Encrypted messaging app **Signal** is now restricting users' ability to take screenshots within its mobile apps, a move it frames as a defense against the "existential threat" of AI-powered surveillance ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/signal-restricts-mobile-screenshots-as-it-confronts-existential-threat-of-ai-surveillance/)).

### Research, Regulation, and… Games?
On the research front, **OpenAI published a blog post** exploring how the metric of "perplexity"—which measures how surprised a model is by new data—could serve as a shared, fundamental measure for tracking AI intelligence and emerging capabilities ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence)).

Global regulation, however, continues to lag. A UN conference in Geneva failed to agree on rules for autonomous weapons systems, or "killer robots," concluding a week of talks with only an agreement to "start discussing options" in the future ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039554/un-regulations-killer-robots)). In the corporate world, **Anthropic's recent IPO** has sparked debate on whether its public company structure and immense valuation ($90 billion) are compatible with its founding mission to prioritize AI safety ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1038701/anthropic-ipo-ai-safety-openai-nvidia-structure)).

And in a lighter note, **Chess.com** is expanding far beyond its namesake game, launching a new "Classic Games" hub featuring backgammon, Go, mahjong, sudoku, and several others ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1038099/chess-classic-games-expansion-ai)).

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of an AI ecosystem maturing—and confronting its contradictions. The technology is simultaneously becoming more physically conspicuous (in data centers) and more personally intimate (in our texts), even as the frameworks to govern its most powerful and dangerous applications remain stuck in neutral. The backlash against AI-generated content in games and the scramble for digital identity control suggest that public acceptance is not a given; it must be earned through transparency and respect for creative and personal sovereignty.
