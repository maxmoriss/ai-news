The march of AI into the mundane continues. **Chatbots are now taking your fast-food orders**, with McDonald's and Wendy's leading the charge. While pitched as a solution to labor shortages, the rollout has been bumpy, highlighting the gap between controlled demos and the chaotic reality of a real drive-thru lane. This is just the beginning of AI seeping into routine customer service interactions. [Source: The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/column/928096/chatbots-ai-drive-thru-mcdonalds-wendys)

Meanwhile, the **vibes in the broader AI industry are reportedly souring**. A new analysis points to a growing divide between the "haves" (well-funded giants with vast compute resources) and "have-nots" (smaller startups and researchers). The skyrocketing costs of training and running cutting-edge models are creating a significant barrier to entry, potentially stifling innovation and centralizing power. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/the-haves-and-have-nots-of-the-ai-gold-rush/)

The scientific community is taking a stand against low-effort AI use. The pre-print repository **ArXiv will now ban authors for a year if they submit papers that are purely AI-generated**. This move aims to curb the influx of nonsensical or plagiarized manuscripts and uphold research integrity, though it raises questions about how such submissions will be detected. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/research-repository-arxiv-will-ban-authors-for-a-year-if-they-let-ai-do-all-the-work/)

In corporate news, **OpenAI is shaking up its product leadership**. Co-founder Greg Brockman is reportedly taking direct charge of product strategy as the company considers merging its flagship ChatGPT with its code-generation tool, Codex. This signals a push towards more integrated and powerful AI assistants. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/openai-co-founder-greg-brockman-reportedly-takes-charge-of-product-strategy/)

On the consumer hardware front, **Sony is defending its AI camera tech**. After social media mockery of its AI Camera Assistant's suggested edits, the company clarified that the feature doesn't automatically alter photos but offers real-time compositional suggestions. It's a case study in managing expectations for AI features that augment, rather than replace, human skill. [Source: The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/932133/sony-xperia-1-xiii-ai-camera-assistant)

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of AI in its awkward adolescence. It's powerful enough to be deployed everywhere—from your burger order to your smartphone camera—but the results are often clumsy, raising practical and ethical concerns. The simultaneous crackdown on AI-generated research and the warning of an industry power consolidation suggest a growing urgency to establish guardrails and ensure this technology develops responsibly, not just rapidly.
