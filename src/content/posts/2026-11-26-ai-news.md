### 🔒 Nvidia Under Pressure on Multiple Fronts
The chip giant is navigating a complex web of regulatory and internal challenges. The U.S. government's tightening controls have led the company to **[pause AI chip shipments to several Southeast Asian nations](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/nvidia-hits-pause-on-chip-shipments-to-southeast-asia/)**. Separately, S&P Global Ratings is **[considering a credit downgrade for Nvidia](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/sp-considers-nvidia-credit-downgrade-over-governance-and-key-man-risks/)**, citing governance and "key man" risks tied to CEO Jensen Huang. Those risks were underscored by a report detailing how **[Huang broke the company's own code of conduct](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/jensen-huang-broke-nvidias-code-of-conduct-and-his-employees-may-not-forget-it/)**, raising questions about corporate culture. Huang and the company also continue to face **[questions about the Megaspeed scandal](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/the-questions-jensen-huang-and-nvidia-will-still-have-to-answer-about-megaspeed/)** and what they knew about diverted chip shipments.

### 🛡️ State AGs Mobilize Against Data Sale in Bankruptcy
In a rare show of unity, a **coalition of 42 state attorneys general** is attempting to block Character.AI from selling users' private chat logs as part of its Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings. The AGs argue the proposed sale would auction off **["the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/)**, including data from 2.1 million minors. The move, covered by both **[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/)** and **[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1042620/character-ai-sale-state-ags-block-selling-users-chats)**, represents a major regulatory challenge to how sensitive AI user data is handled in corporate distress.

### ⚡ The Physical Limits of the AI Boom
The insatiable demand for compute is running into real-world constraints. Building AI data centers is becoming **[more difficult and expensive](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/ai-data-centers-are-becoming-harder-to-build-and-neoclouds-are-feeling-it/)**, with Nvidia taking a hard line on financing only near-complete projects. One proposed solution is looking upward—way upward. The FCC has **[authorized SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million AI chip-equipped satellites](https://www.theverge.com/science/1042278/spacex-fcc-starmind-ai-space-data-centers)** for its "Starmind" space-based data center initiative.

---
**Editorial Take:** Today's news paints a picture of an industry hitting its adolescence, facing consequences and scrutiny after a period of breakneck growth. The simultaneous pressure on Nvidia (from governments, markets, and internal culture) and the unified legal action against Character.AI signal that the "move fast" era is giving way to one of accountability. The physical constraints on data center construction and the extreme solution of space-based compute highlight that the AI revolution isn't just about software—it's a massive, resource-intensive industrial project bumping against the limits of our planet.
