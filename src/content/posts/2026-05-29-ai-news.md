### **The Big Money & The Big Leagues**
The AI funding landscape reached a new stratosphere with news that **Anthropic has raised a staggering $65 billion**, nearing a $1 trillion valuation ahead of a potential IPO. This round, likely its last as a private company, underscores the immense capital required to compete at the frontier. In a related infrastructure drama, **Elon Musk is publicly contesting the length of a compute deal** between SpaceX and Anthropic, suggesting xAI's arrangement may be short-term, despite contractual language indicating otherwise. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/anthropic-raises-65-billion-nears-1t-valuation-ahead-of-ipo/) | [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/how-long-is-anthropics-lease-with-spacex-opinions-vary/)

Meanwhile, enterprise AI is proving its business value. The enterprise search startup **Glean has seen its revenue top $300M**, tripling annually by positioning its AI tools as a way for companies to cut costs and find efficiency. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/gleans-top-line-crosses-300m-as-ai-budget-cutting-becomes-its-major-selling-point/)

### **Building Blocks: Chips, Agents & Infrastructure**
As models grow, bottlenecks shift. South Korean chip startup **XCENA has raised $135M** on a contrarian bet: AI's biggest limitation isn't compute power, but memory bandwidth. Their architecture aims to solve the "memory wall" problem slowing down massive AI workloads. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/29/xcena-secures-135m-at-570m-valuation-betting-on-memory-as-ais-real-bottleneck/)

The software layer is evolving just as fast. **Anthropic released its new Opus 4.8 model**, featuring a "Dynamic Workflow" tool for coordinating swarms of AI subagents, pushing further into autonomous, multi-step task execution. **Asana acquired no-code agent builder StackAI**, aiming to bake sophisticated AI workflows directly into its project management platform. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/anthropic-releases-opus-4-8-with-new-dynamic-workflow-tool/) | [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/asana-acquires-no-code-agent-builder-stack-ai/)

This agent-centric future is reshaping the internet itself. A major report argues **the cloud is being rebuilt for machines**, with AWS and Cloudflare redesigning infrastructure for an era where AI agents, not humans, generate the majority of network traffic. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/the-internet-is-being-rebuilt-for-machines/)

### **Research, Risks & Real-World Tests**
On the policy and safety front, **OpenAI has launched "Rosalind Biodefense,"** a program to provide vetted U.S. government partners and developers with trusted access to its GPT-Rosalind model for advancing biodefense and pandemic preparedness. [Source: OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/strengthening-societal-resilience-with-rosalind-biodefense)

In the wild, AI's integration is hitting some snags. A developer, fed up with so-called "vibe coders" over-relying on AI, **sneaked a malicious prompt injection into a popular coding library** that instructed AI agents to delete application output—a stark warning about the security risks of uncritically accepting AI-generated code. [Source: Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/05/fed-up-with-vibe-coders-dev-sneaks-data-nuking-prompt-injection-into-their-code/)

Other startups are taking creative approaches to gathering the real-world data needed to advance. **Shift, an AI training startup, is offering free home cleaning** in exchange for permission to film the cleaners, using the footage to train future domestic robots. [Source: The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/939765/ai-training-data-startup-shift-free-cleaning)

**Editorial Take:** Today's news paints a picture of an industry in a hyper-growth phase, hurtling towards a future of autonomous AI agents. The monumental funding, the race for new hardware paradigms, and the rebuilding of core internet infrastructure all point in one direction: AI is moving from a tool used by people to a foundational layer that operates independently. The critical question emerging isn't just about capability, but about control—who governs these systems, how we secure them, and what data they're built upon. The prompt injection hack and the home-cleaning data grab are early tremors of the governance and ethical challenges this agentic future will bring.
