### The Human Cost of Automation
As companies race to integrate AI, a concerning trend is emerging: a disconnect between decision-makers and the reality of the work they seek to automate. Box founder Aaron Levie has coined a term for this: **["AI psychosis"](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/does-your-ceo-have-ai-psychosis-aaron-levie-thinks-most-of-them-do/)**, where leaders who least understand a job are most eager to replace it with an AI agent. This is more than theoretical; productivity platform ClickUp recently cut 22% of its workforce in favor of AI agents. The human impact of this push is also evident in the coding world, where **[coders are refusing to work without AI assistance](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/29/coders-are-refusing-to-work-without-ai-and-that-could-come-back-to-bite-them/)**, though researchers warn this may lead to faster but buggier, lower-quality code. Even Cognition's Scott Wu, creator of the famed Devin AI coding agent, **[says his tool shouldn't replace humans](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/29/cognitions-scott-wu-says-ai-coding-agents-shouldnt-replace-humans/)**.

### Hardware & Startup Shakeups
The relentless demand for AI compute is fueling a boom in alternative chip architectures. Following Nvidia's massive $20B offer (which wasn't an acquisition), AI chip startup **Groq is reportedly seeking to raise $650M** to pivot more heavily into AI inference, [per a new report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/29/after-nvidias-20b-not-acqui-hire-ai-chip-startup-groq-reportedly-raising-650m/). Meanwhile, another startup, **XCENA, just raised $135M** on a contrarian bet: [that memory, not compute, is AI's true bottleneck](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/29/xcena-secures-135m-at-570m-valuation-betting-on-memory-as-ais-real-bottleneck/). In the world of big tech, Google showcased a slew of **[new demos for its Gemini Omni and Gemini 3.5 models](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-omni-3-5-videos/)**, and OpenAI is trying to woo enterprises with new **[governance frameworks for scaling safe AI](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/scaling-safe-enterprise-ai-openai-governance-frameworks/)**.

### AI's Stranger Applications
From mundane chores to wildlife watching, AI's applications are getting peculiar. A startup called **Shift is cleaning New Yorkers' homes for free** with one major string attached: [they want to film you doing chores](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/940007/ai-companies-will-pay-for-robot-training-data) to gather training data for domestic robots. On the lighter side, an **[AI-powered bird feeder from Kiwibit](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/29/kiwibits-ai-powered-bird-feeder-is-my-new-backyard-buddy/)** can identify species for your personal digital collection. But the creepier side of synthetic media persists, with reports of **[AI grifters creating fake Black personas](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/938844/ai-tiktok-shop-blackface-shein-dropshipping)** to shill products on TikTok Shop, raising serious ethical alarms.

### Odds & Ends
In security news, authorities have **[dismantled a massive botnet of over 17 million devices](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/05/botnet-of-more-than-17-million-devices-dismantled/)** tied to a Russia-based proxy network. And if you're feeling overwhelmed by AI jargon, TechCrunch has published a handy **[glossary of common AI terms](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/29/artificial-intelligence-definition-glossary-hallucinations-guide-to-common-ai-terms/)** to help you keep up.

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of an industry in the messy, often painful, adolescence of its AI adoption. The tension is palpable: between the seductive efficiency of automation and the tangible human cost, between groundbreaking hardware bets and dystopian data harvesting schemes. The term "AI psychosis" is particularly resonant—it captures the dangerous allure of a solution in search of a problem, driven more by hype than understanding. As the technology becomes more capable, the need for ethical guardrails and human oversight becomes not a hindrance, but the essential ingredient for sustainable progress.
