## The money is still enormous, but the assumptions are wobbling

OpenAI is reportedly raising another $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/openai-reportedly-raises-an-additional-11-billion-at-a-1-25-trillion-valuation/), and [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029380/openai-1-25-trillion-investment-valuation) notes that the round lands just as rival Anthropic approaches its IPO — a reminder that the AI capital cycle is not cooling yet.

But not everyone is convinced the spending curve will keep rising. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/ramp-data-shows-ai-spending-stalling-just-as-anthropic-nears-its-ipo/) reports Ramp data showing AI spending stalling even as usage climbs, a warning sign for investors betting on uninterrupted hypergrowth.

The physical side of the boom is also getting messy. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/crusoe-sells-troubled-wyoming-data-center-project-to-google/) says Crusoe sold its troubled Wyoming data center project to Google, highlighting how hard it is to build the massive facilities needed to feed AI ambitions.

## Data centers are becoming a ballot-box problem

The politics of AI infrastructure are no longer just a tech-policy footnote. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/how-a-blue-wave-midterm-might-reshape-the-ai-industry/) examines how a possible Democratic wave could reshape the AI industry after voters overwhelmingly backed stricter data-center rules in 41 ballot measures.

A companion [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/video/after-the-midterms-is-the-ai-industry-in-for-a-rough-two-years/) video asks whether the industry now faces a rough two years as local politics, energy costs, and environmental concerns turn data centers into a bipartisan flashpoint.

For background, [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/902546/data-centers-ai-energy-power-grids-controversy) has an ongoing explainer on AI data centers, energy, power grids, utility bills, and community fights — the physical constraints that could slow the AI race more than model training ever will.

## Nvidia, China, and the theater of export controls

The export-control regime looks even shakier after [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029483/nvidia-ai-chips-china-blacklist-government) reported that the US allowed Nvidia to sell chips to an empty office in China, raising questions about Commerce Department and Nvidia due diligence.

The espionage angle is even more uncomfortable: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1029338/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-china-spy) says China’s intelligence agency reportedly recruited a Megaspeed executive to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and push for access to the company’s most advanced chips.

## AI is breaking cybersecurity before it fixes it

If you want a sobering overview of where security stands, [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1029038/ai-cybersecurity-hackers-data-breach-dark-age-alex-stamos) hosts Alex Stamos discussing how AI has already wrecked the cybersecurity world, from faster breaches to new attack economics.

That warning is not hypothetical. [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/chainalysis-says-hackers-stole-credentials-and-tokens-for-all-of-its-customers/) reports Chainalysis said hackers stole credentials and tokens for all of its customers in a “multifront cyberattack” that included AI agents and on-path data theft.

And tooling failures are creating new exposure. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/hundreds-of-artex-ai-hacking-tools-found-publicly-exposed-to-the-web/) says security researchers found hundreds of servers hosting the penetration-testing tool Artex publicly exposed to the web, effectively handing attackers access to an AI hacking toolkit.

## The self-improvement problem is no longer theoretical

The most consequential story may be [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1029126/anthropic-metr-report-accelerating-ai-development) reporting on a METR investigation into Anthropic using AI to build more powerful AI, with models improving roughly twice as quickly as a year ago and little sign of slowing down. That is exactly the kind of acceleration researchers are warning regulators, companies, and investors to take seriously.

## Products that feel closer, and research that feels farther away

On the consumer side, [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1029489/nyt-cooking-ai-assistant-review) reviews NYT Cooking’s AI assistant: great for rescuing weeknight chicken, dangerous for cake. The lesson is that AI can be delightfully helpful in narrow contexts and spectacularly wrong in delicate ones.

Meta is pushing its Muse assistant into more app-like territory. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/meta-introduces-spaces-a-new-way-to-collaborate-with-your-muse-agent/) says Meta introduced Spaces, a new way to collaborate with your Muse agent, while [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1028871/meta-muse-ai-spaces) explains that users can build custom experiences like shopping hubs, school dashboards, music trackers, trivia games, and more.

Amazon is also trying to make its assistant more useful. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/after-blocking-muse-amazon-now-lets-users-text-alexa-to-help-them-shop/) reports that after blocking Muse, Amazon now lets users text Alexa to help them shop, rolling out to millions in coming weeks.

Smaller models are getting more capable. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/underdog-shows-off-a-32mb-speech-model-in-new-demo/) highlights Underdog’s 32MB speech model that can generate both speech and music, a useful signal that efficiency work is still moving fast.

Robotics and simulation continue to attract money. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/physicl-raises-10m-to-make-the-digital-world-real-enough-for-robots/) says Physicl raised $10M to make the digital world real enough for robots, aiming at the stubborn sim-to-real gap.

And science is quietly getting a new lever: [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/how-to-predict-molecule-behavior-with-scant-data-1106) describes CheMeleon, a machine-learning method that predicts molecule behavior from scant data, potentially reducing expensive experimentation in drug discovery and materials science.

**The day’s theme:** AI is finally being priced like critical infrastructure, governed like critical infrastructure, and attacked like critical infrastructure — while its own developers use it to build the next version faster than anyone can write the rules.
