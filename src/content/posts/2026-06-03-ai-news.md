### The Cost of AI Adoption Hits Home
The initial "use it freely" phase of enterprise AI is ending. **Uber** has capped employee AI spending after blowing through its annual budget in just four months, and **Walmart** is limiting use of its internal "Code Puppy" assistant after unanticipated demand led to soaring costs. These moves highlight the growing pains as generative AI transitions from experimental toy to operational tool, forcing a new focus on ROI and cost management.

### Regulatory Winds Shift Publisher Control
In a significant move, **U.K. regulators** have ruled that Google must offer website publishers a tool to opt out of having their content used in generative AI search features like AI Overviews. Google will test the tool in the U.K. before a global rollout. This gives content creators more control over how their work trains and fuels AI products, potentially setting a precedent for other regions.

### Building and Monitoring the AI Future
On the development front, **Microsoft** had a busy Build conference, announcing a new tool called [Adaptive Spec-driven Scoring](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/02/new-microsoft-tool-lets-devs-spin-up-ai-behavior-tests-using-text-descriptions/) that lets developers create AI behavior tests using simple text descriptions. Meanwhile, as AI agents proliferate, someone needs to watch them: monitoring startup **Coralogix** raised a $200M Series F at a $1.6B valuation to build the "observability layer" for AI agents.

In research, **MIT** unveiled a new training dataset called [ChartNet](https://news.mit.edu/2026/mit-researchers-teach-ai-models-to-interpret-charts-0603) designed to teach AI models to interpret charts and figures more accurately, a key skill for business and scientific analysis.

### Startup Spotlight: Voice AI for Overlooked Markets
Two founders left Goldman Sachs and Meta to build a voice AI startup focused on Africa and the Middle East. Their proprietary stack is now handling over 17,000 calls per day, serving markets often neglected by larger tech firms. This underscores that impactful AI innovation isn't just about the biggest models, but also about solving specific, high-need problems.

### Quick Hits
*   **Meta** has made its AI agent for WhatsApp Business available globally, with businesses charged based on token usage.
*   **Google** is rolling out fake call detection features to help protect users from AI deepfake voice scams.
*   **Martin Scorsese** is using AI for storyboarding, becoming an unlikely advocate for the tech's creative utility.

**Editorial:** Today's news paints a picture of an industry entering a more mature, but more complicated, phase. The unchecked spending and "move fast" ethos are colliding with budgetary realities and regulatory frameworks. The focus is shifting from pure capability to control, cost, and compliance—signs that AI is becoming a standard, if demanding, part of the business toolkit.
