### The Running Tally: AI as the Go-To Rationale for Layoffs
A new report from TechCrunch is keeping a running list of major tech companies that have announced significant layoffs in 2026, specifically citing AI as a factor. The latest addition is work management platform [Monday.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/25/the-running-list-major-tech-layoffs-in-2026-where-employers-cited-ai/), joining a list of over 20 others. The trend highlights a corporate narrative where "AI-driven efficiency" is increasingly synonymous with workforce reduction, raising questions about the technology's immediate human cost in the pursuit of automation and profit.

### Libraries Host Viral "Avoiding AI" Workshops
In a stark counter-movement, public libraries across the country are seeing unprecedented demand for "[Avoiding AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/25/librarians-are-hosting-viral-avoiding-ai-workshops-for-people-who-are-fed-up-with-big-tech/)" workshops. Led by librarians, these sessions teach practical skills for navigating daily life—from web search to document editing—without relying on AI tools from major tech companies. The viral popularity of these workshops points to a growing public sentiment of distrust, fatigue, and a desire for human-centric alternatives in an increasingly automated digital landscape.

### A Fallen Power Line Exposes AI's Infrastructure Vulnerability
The massive energy appetite of AI data centers is a known issue, but a new report details a critical vulnerability: their poor response to grid instability. A [recent incident in Northern Virginia](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/25/one-fallen-power-line-exposed-a-growing-ai-data-center-problem-heres-how-to-fix-it/)—a major data center hub—saw a single fallen transmission line nearly trigger cascading failures, as data centers' backup generators failed to engage properly. The article argues for urgent fixes, including mandatory "grid-forming" inverter technology for backup systems, to prevent local power disruptions from causing widespread AI service outages and threatening grid reliability for everyone.

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of AI at an inflection point. The technology is mature enough to be a scapegoat for corporate restructuring and pervasive enough to spark organized public resistance. Meanwhile, the physical infrastructure it relies on appears more fragile than assumed. The theme is friction: between AI's promise and its human impact, between its adoption and a growing rejection movement, and between its digital ambitions and the very real-world constraints of energy and engineering.
