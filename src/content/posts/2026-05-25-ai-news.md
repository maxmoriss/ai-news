### The Vatican's Diagnosis of Tech Power
Pope Leo XIV’s first encyclical, *Technologiae in Servitio*, uses artificial intelligence not as its central subject, but as a modern lens to critique enduring issues. The document argues that the fundamental problems are not new: concentrated power, the erosion of democratic institutions, and a technological elite shaping the world to its own advantage. It positions AI as the latest, most potent tool in this long-standing dynamic, calling for a focus on human dignity and the common good over unchecked technological progress. [Read more on TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/the-popes-ai-encyclical-isnt-really-about-ai/).

### Quantum Computing's Funding Quandary
The U.S. government's ambitious, multi-billion dollar bet on quantum computing—including the launch of the nation's first dedicated quantum foundry company—is facing questions over its legality. Critics argue the funding mechanism may circumvent traditional congressional appropriations processes, raising concerns about oversight and the actual need for a state-backed fabrication hub in a still-nascent field. [Read more on Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/05/uss-big-bet-on-quantum-computing-may-not-be-entirely-legal/).

### The Evolving Battle for AI Security
Security challenges are evolving as quickly as AI models themselves. A new report details how hackers are moving beyond simple prompt injections to exploit the crafted "personalities" of chatbots, using social engineering tactics to manipulate them into bypassing safety guidelines. This underscores a broader theme: everyone, from individual users to giants like Google, is navigating AI security in real time during this transitional period. [Read more on The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/column/935545/hackers-ai-chatbots). For a broader look at this ongoing navigation, see [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/24/everyone-is-navigating-ai-security-in-real-time-even-google/).

**Editorial Take:** Today’s stories paint a picture of an industry hitting its adolescence. The lofty ethical warnings from the Vatican, the gritty legal fights over quantum funding, and the novel security exploits against chatbots all point to a single theme: **accountability**. As AI's capabilities mature, the focus is sharpening on who wields its power, who pays for its future, and who is responsible when it goes awry. The era of pure, unexamined innovation is giving way to a more complex phase of governance, security, and societal negotiation.
