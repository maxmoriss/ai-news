### **Google Unveils Gemini 4 Argon with Robotics in Mind**
Google DeepMind has officially launched **Gemini 4 Argon**, its next-generation flagship model family. The company emphasizes a foundation of responsible development, with extensive safety evaluations and red-teaming. A key focus is on real-world applications, with a dedicated variant, **[Gemini 4 Argon for Robotics](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-4-argon-for-robotics/)**, designed to power more capable and helpful robots. The model is positioned as a tool "[built to solve your hardest problems](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-argon/)."

### **Startups Stress-Test the Physical World of AI**
As AI moves beyond the screen, new companies are emerging to manage its real-world risks. **Laplace Intelligence**, a spinout from the University of Michigan, uses AI agents in simulation to red-team robots and self-driving cars before deployment, competing at TechCrunch Disrupt. Meanwhile, **Flox Intelligence** is deploying edge AI to identify wildlife near railways and airports and use species-specific sounds to deter them, aiming to prevent collisions.

### **User Control Takes Center Stage at Meta and Google**
Meta is expanding user control over algorithmic feeds. Following its Instagram launch, the "[Your Algorithm](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/13/facebook-is-giving-users-more-control-over-their-algorithm/)" feature is coming to Facebook, allowing users to directly add or remove topics to shape their feed and Reels recommendations. In a similar vein of personalized AI, **Google Play** now supports the direct launch of private, AI "vibe-coded" Android apps, making it easier for users to build and use personal tools without publishing them.

### **AI Commerce and Security See Major Moves**
The ecosystem for AI-powered shopping is rapidly formalizing. **Shopify** is opening its catalog of over a billion product listings to more third-party AI shopping agents. Concurrently, **American Express** introduced a new framework designed to ensure AI agents only make authorized purchases, addressing a core trust issue in agentic commerce. On the enterprise security front, **Pluto Security** raised $42 million to help companies manage the novel risks introduced by widespread employee access to generative AI tools.

### **In Other News**
*   **Satlyt**, founded by a former Google and SpaceX PM, raised $8M to build open, Android-like software for running AI directly on satellites, after both tech giants passed on the idea.
*   **X** (formerly Twitter) has launched a new subscription bundle called "X Subscription," combining X Premium, Grok AI, and the Cursor developer tool.
*   Actress **Mia Goth** criticized Instagram for allowing a verified but fake AI-generated account, posting a deepfake image of her, to spread misinformation about her personal life.

### **Editorial: The Week of Agency**
Today's news underscores a pivotal theme: the maturation of AI is forcing a shift towards greater human agency and systemic safety. Google's Argon launch isn't just about raw capability but responsible deployment, especially in high-stakes domains like robotics. Meta and Google's user-control features represent a direct response to demands for algorithmic transparency. Meanwhile, the flurry of activity in commerce (Shopify, Amex) and security (Pluto, Laplace) highlights an industry scrambling to build guardrails and infrastructure for an AI-native world. The message is clear: the next phase is about putting humans back in the loop, whether they're tuning a feed, approving a purchase, or ensuring a robot behaves safely.
