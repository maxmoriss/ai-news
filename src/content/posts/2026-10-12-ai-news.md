## Frontier Models Get Bigger at Meta
Meta has officially entered the frontier model race with the release of **Muse Wonder** and **Muse Wonder Full**. These large models are positioned as more capable successors to the lightweight Muse Spark, boasting higher benchmark scores and improved performance on complex tasks like coding. As [The Verge reports](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1009479/meta-muse-wonder-full-ai-model), the company claims they can go "toe-to-toe with the industry's best," signaling Meta's intensified push to compete at the cutting edge of AI development. The launch follows the recent restructuring of its AI division into Superintelligence Labs.

## Microsoft Bets Big on Its Own AI Silicon
Microsoft is reducing its reliance on Nvidia with the announcement of its third-generation in-house AI chip, **Maia 300**. Designed as the "primary AI inference system" for Microsoft's cloud services, the new chip promises improved cost efficiency and better handling of advanced reasoning models and agentic tasks. [According to The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/1009465/microsoft-maia-300-chip-improvements-rollout), this move is a key part of Microsoft's strategy to control its AI infrastructure and optimize performance for its own sprawling suite of AI products.

## The "Superintelligence" Rebrand Gains Ground
A political effort to rebrand artificial intelligence is finding surprising purchase in tech circles. Following former President Donald Trump's push to rename AI "super intelligence," several prominent Silicon Valley leaders are reportedly adopting the term. [The Verge notes](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1009406/silicon-valley-is-falling-in-line-with-trumps-attempt-to-rename-ai) that while the broader public hasn't embraced the shift, its adoption within influential tech circles could signal a new phase in the politicization of AI terminology.

**Editorial Take:** Today's news underscores a sector in a state of simultaneous consolidation and fragmentation. Giants like Meta and Microsoft are consolidating power—one through massive new models, the other through proprietary silicon—to secure their positions in the foundational layer of AI. Meanwhile, a constellation of startups is fragmenting into highly specialized verticals, from architecture to weather prediction, proving there's still ample room for innovation in applied AI. The curious adoption of "super intelligence" terminology by some tech elites, however, serves as a reminder that the development of this technology is never far from political and rhetorical battles.
