### **The Nvidia Leadership Crisis Widens**
The fallout from Jensen Huang's ouster continues to dominate the conversation. Palmer Luckey argued that China is "thrilled" by the departure, claiming it weakens America, and called for Huang's reinstation [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/palmer-luckey-thinks-china-is-thrilled-to-have-jensen-out-so-he-wants-the-ousted-nvidia-ceo-reinstated/). Sam Altman echoed the sentiment, stating it's "hard to imagine the AI industry without Jensen" [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/openai-ceo-sam-altman-says-its-hard-to-imagine-the-ai-industry-without-jensen/). Meanwhile, interim CEO Colette Kress clarified Nvidia's financing strategy for "neoclouds," stating the chip giant won't be on the hook for loans unless facilities are built and equipped with its hardware [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-clarifies-when-it-will-and-wont-keep-financing-neoclouds/). Analysts are left wondering what's next for the profit-generating behemoth [**(source)**](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1035924/nvidia-visualized-revenue-profit-earnings-after-huang).

### **Security Warnings and Policy Moves**
Beyond boardroom drama, stark warnings emerged about AI's threat landscape. Anduril founder Palmer Luckey cautioned that AI-powered cyberattacks are rapidly outpacing defenses, urging a sprint to catch up [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/palmer-luckey-warns-ai-powered-cyberattacks-are-outpacing-defenses-its-time-to-start-running/). In the financial sector, Citi joined other banks in stating customers who use unauthorized AI agents for banking are on their own if they get hacked [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/). On the policy front, US lawmakers are pushing for new protections against online harms, including deepfakes, before the year ends [**(source)**](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1036935/congress-forced-arbitration-deepfakes-defiance-act). In a geopolitical move, former President Trump visited the Philippines to promote a new AI and data center hub [**(source)**](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1036749/trump-ai-chip-data-center-us-philippines-pax-silica).

### **New Products and Creative Tools**
Google had a busy day on the product front. It launched **Gemini 4 Neon**, a new model family focused on efficiency for on-device and cost-sensitive applications [**(source)**](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-neon/), and made the Gemini app available on lower-memory Android devices [**(source)**](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-lower-memory-devices/). It also gave its AI-powered Search feature a new "incognito mode" for private conversations [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/googles-ai-feature-in-search-gets-its-own-incognito-mode/). Separately, DeepMind released **Genie 4**, its latest world model for generating interactive environments [**(source)**](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-is-now-available/). Snapchat announced developers can now build AR Lenses using AI coding assistants like Claude Code by simply describing what they want [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/snapchat-now-lets-developers-build-ar-lenses-with-ai-coding-assistants-like-claude-code-and-cursor/).

### **Robotics, Research, and Startups**
In robotics, MIT researchers unveiled a featherweight flying robot that carries its own sensors and computers, a step toward real-world applications like agricultural monitoring [**(source)**](https://news.mit.edu/2026/featherweight-flying-robot-carries-its-own-sensors-and-computers-1118). Disney showcased new AI-powered Star Wars animatronics that can interact spontaneously with park guests using Amazon's cloud AI [**(source)**](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1036729/disney-star-wars-anzellans-animatronic-amazon-aws-cloud-ai). In startup funding, **Unsiloed AI** raised $7.5M to help AI agents understand complex enterprise documents [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/felicis-leads-7-5m-bet-on-unsiloed-ai-to-make-enterprise-documents-usable-by-ai-agents/), and India's **Humyn Labs** secured $47M to supply training data for U.S. robotics companies [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/accel-backs-indias-humyn-labs-to-supply-training-data-needed-by-u-s-robotics-ai-developers/).

### **Altman's Meta-Moment**
In a lighter note, Sam Altman reacted to news that Andrew Garfield will portray him in an upcoming movie, quipping that it's not in the "top 10 weirdest things" about his life [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/sam-altman-says-his-life-is-so-weird-that-hes-unfazed-by-andrew-garfield-portraying-him-in-upcoming-movie/). He also reiterated warnings about the need to keep advanced AI under human control [**(source)**](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/sam-altman-warns-against-a-future-where-ai-not-humans-calls-the-shots/).

**Editorial Take:** Today's news paints a picture of an industry at an inflection point. The intense focus on one leader's absence reveals a surprising fragility beneath AI's explosive growth. Simultaneously, the field is maturing: the warnings about cybersecurity and policy debates are the growing pains of a technology moving from lab to world, while new tools are democratizing creation from AR to robotics. The paradox is clear—AI's potential is boundless, but its foundations, from leadership to security, need shoring up.
