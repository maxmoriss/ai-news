### **Disney's Strategic AI Hiring Spree**
In a move highlighting the entertainment giant's serious push into AI, **Disney has agreed to hire the majority of the workforce from bankrupt chatbot startup Character.AI**. The deal follows Disney's recent $20 million acquisition of some of the startup's assets and the hiring of its former CEO, Karandeep Anand, as Disney's first-ever Chief Technology Officer. This mass hiring signals Disney's intent to build internal AI expertise rapidly, leveraging talent that specialized in conversational AI and character-driven interactions.
[Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/disney-agrees-to-hire-the-majority-of-character-ais-workforce/)

### **The Web Pushes Back Against AI Agents**
The promise of autonomous AI agents that can browse and act on the web is running into a major roadblock: website owners. **A growing number of sites are actively blocking these agents**, using updated `robots.txt` directives and other technical measures to deny them access. Companies like OpenAI, Meta, and Google are promoting agent capabilities, but publishers and service providers are concerned about scraping, server load, and potential misuse. This tension points to a looming battle over the rules of engagement for the next generation of AI on the open web.
[Source: The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013195/ai-agents-blocked-web)

### **Meta Quietly Edits Controversial Data Terms**
Under scrutiny, **Meta has removed specific language from its commercial terms that suggested it could scrape content from any website, including news publishers, to train its AI models**. The deleted clause had stated Meta could use such content to "provide, develop, improve, and maintain" its services. Following a report by The Verge and subsequent backlash, a Meta spokesperson insisted this was not a change in policy but a clarification, claiming the company does not crawl the web for AI training. The episode highlights the intense sensitivity around data sourcing for AI.
[Sources: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/meta-removes-language-that-raised-scraping-and-ai-training-concerns/) | [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1013409/meta-removes-language-that-may-have-allowed-it-to-scrape-news-sites)

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories sketch a clear theme of **consolidation and confrontation**. On one side, established players like Disney are consolidating AI talent and assets from the startup world. On the other, a confrontation is brewing over the fundamental resources of the AI era: data and web access. Meta's quiet retreat on scraping terms and the web's revolt against AI agents show that the industry's "move fast" ethos is increasingly clashing with the rights and defenses of content creators and platform owners. The rules for the next phase of AI are being written in real time, and not solely by the tech giants.
