## Apple’s AI ecosystem takes shape

- Apple used its latest earnings to paint itself as the AI company that can capitalize on the boom without the crushing infrastructure bill its competitors are facing; iPhone and Mac growth helped deliver its best-ever fiscal year for revenue and earnings per share, while Apple leans on both its own AI and partner models. [source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025245/apple-earnings-q4-ai)
- The company is also making Siri more useful outside its own walls: in the iOS 27.3 update rolling out Tuesday, developers can integrate Siri AI into “all apps,” letting the assistant use personal context, natural-language prompts, and on-screen awareness. [source](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025694/apple-siri-ai-third-party-apps)
- On the wrist, watchOS 27.3 developer beta introduces Audio Intelligence features, including Siri Recap for ambient conversation summaries and Live Rewind for text transcripts of the last 15 minutes. [source](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025002/apple-watch-audio-intelligence-watchos-beta)
- Meanwhile, Apple is tightening age controls around AI-adjacent intimacy: new App Store rules require 18+ ratings for dating apps and AI companions that offer sexual or romantic content. [source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/apple-restricts-dating-apps-and-ai-companions-to-users-18-and-up/)

## The economics of AI are getting awkward

- Fal, a five-year-old AI inference startup, raised a $1.3 billion round led by Bessemer and doubled its valuation to $16 billion as demand for inference surges. [source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/ai-inference-startup-fal-doubles-its-valuation-to-16b-as-demand-surges/)
- Anthropic’s story is less rosy: at a moment when compute margins should be improving, they’re reportedly getting worse, underscoring how hard it remains to turn frontier-model scale into durable economics. [source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-still-grappling-with-the-economics-of-compute/)
- OpenAI is trying to soften the politics of its buildout by quietly renaming data centers “compute campuses,” betting that a friendlier label might ease growing bipartisan opposition to loud, resource-hungry facilities. [source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025242/openai-quietly-renames-data-centers-as-compute-campuses)
- Infrastructure policy is also moving in research land: MIT.nano and the Northeast Microelectronics Coalition brought facility leaders together to explore making shared-access microelectronics facilities more connected. [source](https://news.mit.edu/2026/building-connections-across-research-infrastructure-1103)

## Search, discovery, and the schoolhouse

- OpenAI says ChatGPT is catching up to Google in search, claiming the volume of search queries run through ChatGPT now equals 20 percent of Google’s volume—a sign that “Ask Chat” is becoming a real consumer habit. [source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025482/openai-says-chatgpt-is-catching-up-to-google-in-search)
- Netflix is experimenting with a new “AI Discover” tab that surfaces themed recommendations—“Something Light,” “Family Movie Night,” “Quick Watches”—and will eventually let users ask for content based on mood. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/netflixs-new-ai-discover-tab-helps-you-find-something-to-watch-based-on-your-mood/), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/streaming/1025029/netflix-ai-discover-recommendations)
- Google’s education changes are more coercive: schools that disable AI features may lose access to Google Search entirely because AI is now integrated into the search experience, meaning K-12 accounts could have Gemini tools and Search turned off together. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/google-will-let-schools-disable-its-ai-tools-but-not-without-sacrificing-search/), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025411/google-turn-off-search-ai-features)

## Research tools, security, and policy watch

- Google DeepMind says its Antigravity and Cheminformatics Skills can accelerate drug discovery workflows, compound search, and the design of novel rigidified macrocycles through agentic cheminformatics. [source](https://deepmind.google/blog/agentic-cheminformatics-with-google-antigravity/)
- OpenAI also highlighted how ChatGPT and Codex are being used to accelerate engineering workflows in the physical world, including CAD, PCB design, and simulation. [source](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-engineering-for-the-physical-world)
- Security researchers are asking whether Microsoft bears responsibility after the UEFI bootkit BlackX infected fully patched Windows machines, exploiting a lack of clarity around Secure Boot protections. [source](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/uefi-bootkit-infected-fully-patched-windows-machines-is-microsoft-to-blame/)
- The 2026 midterm elections are shaping up as a tech-policy flashpoint, with affordability dominating while questions around AI regulation, privacy, and platform power remain in play. [source](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1020640/midterm-elections-2026-all-the-news)
- And a reminder that AI hype doesn’t make every platform problem go away: indie game developers are missing launch dates because Steam’s approval process is backed up. [source](https://www.theverge.com/games/1025596/valve-steam-indie-games-missing-launch-dates)

## The day’s theme

The throughline today is control: Apple is trying to control how AI shows up on devices and in apps, OpenAI and Google are fighting for the search box, schools are being forced to choose between AI and basic information access, and startups are discovering that inference demand and compute economics can move in opposite directions. AI is no longer just a model race; it’s a distribution, infrastructure, and policy race.
