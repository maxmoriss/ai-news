**Google's Bumpy AI Ride**
The rollout of new AI features is rarely smooth, and Google is providing a masterclass. Users discovered that searching for the word ["disregard"](https://www.theverge.com/tech/936176/google-ai-overviews-search-disregard) caused its new AI Overviews to malfunction, outputting generic chatbot responses instead of search summaries. The issue became so pronounced that, for a time, you [could no longer effectively Google the word](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/you-can-no-longer-google-the-word-disregard/). In more positive hardware news, TechCrunch got hands-on with Google's [prototype AI glasses](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/we-tried-googles-ai-glasses-and-theyre-almost-there/), finding the Gemini-powered translation and navigation overlays impressive and "almost there." Meanwhile, on the creative front, a Verge writer explored the wild capabilities of Google's new ["anything-to-anything" AI model](https://www.theverge.com/tech/936507/gemini-omni-hands-on-deepfake-ai-video), using it to create deepfake adventures for a child's stuffed animal.

**The High-Stakes AI Economy**
The financial engines of AI are roaring, but questions about sustainability and integrity are growing. A TechCrunch investigation reveals how [VCs and founders are using inflated revenue metrics](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/how-vcs-and-founders-use-inflated-arr-to-kingmake-ai-startups/) to "kingmake" AI startups, with investors often turning a blind eye. The hardware side is paying off handsomely for some; Samsung memory chip employees, critical to the AI supply chain, have [negotiated average annual bonuses of $340,000](https://www.theverge.com/tech/936002/samsung-memory-chip-employees-deal-strike-bonus) following strike threats. In a galactic-scale financial move, SpaceX has filed to go public with a [valuation target of $1.75 trillion](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/elon-musk-cant-hear-you-over-the-sound-of-his-1-75-trillion-ipo/) and a staggering $28 trillion total addressable market claim, a filing that [requires "a little faith"](https://techcrunch.com/video/spacex-files-to-go-public-and-the-math-requires-a-little-faith/). Notably, the filing highlights a shift in Elon Musk's energy vision, with xAI reportedly [giving up on Earth-based solar power](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/23/elon-musk-has-given-up-on-solar-power-on-earth/) in favor of natural gas, while SpaceX focuses on orbital data centers.

**Policy, Security, and Ethical Flashpoints**
As AI integrates deeper into society, it's creating novel legal and ethical dilemmas. The NTSB was forced to temporarily block access to its docket system after people used AI to [resurrect the voices of deceased pilots](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/ai-is-being-used-to-resurrect-the-voices-of-dead-pilots/) from cockpit recording spectrograms. In the literary world, the system was caught off guard as a story shortlisted for the Commonwealth Prize [appears to have been written by AI](https://www.theverge.com/tech/936073/ai-writing-granta-commonwealth-prize), raising questions about artistic integrity. On the security front, a hacker group dubbed TeamPCP is conducting an [unprecedented campaign of poisoning open-source code](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/05/a-hacker-group-is-poisoning-open-source-code-at-an-unprecedented-scale/), with GitHub as its latest victim. In legal news, Texas AG Ken Paxton has [sued Meta](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/05/texas-ag-sues-meta-over-claims-that-whatsapp-doesnt-provide-end-to-end-encryption/), claiming WhatsApp's encryption claims are false—a case critics say lacks factual support.

**Editorial: The Disregard Gap**
Today's news embodies the "disregard gap" in AI development: the chasm between breakneck innovation and its practical, ethical, and societal consequences. Google's AI breaks on a simple word while its models enable eerily convincing deepfakes. Startups are crowned with fuzzy math while the hardware they depend on fuels labor disputes. We use AI to reconstruct the final moments of the dead, yet our literary prizes aren't prepared to detect its voice. The industry's gaze is split between the glittering promise of glasses that translate the world and the sobering reality of poisoned code and legal challenges. The lesson isn't to slow down, but to build with a wider lens—one that sees the word "disregard" not as a bug, but as a warning.
