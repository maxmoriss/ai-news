### OpenAI Doubles Down on Commerce and Agents
OpenAI is pushing AI deeper into the consumer shopping experience. The company is **rolling out virtual try-on features for ChatGPT**, allowing users to see how clothing and accessories look on their own photos and save favorites. This follows another case study showing how **Albertsons Companies is using ChatGPT Enterprise** to reimagine retail operations. In a more theoretical post, the company also published an essay titled ["The eternal complement"](https://openai.com/index/the-eternal-complement), arguing that advanced AI's greatest impact may be in handling the routine work behind breakthrough ideas, supercharging execution.

On the agent front, **OpenAI unveiled "Dots,"** its new AI agent platform powered by GPT-6 Astra, in a direct shot at Meta's popular Muse agent, according to [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1003399/meta-openai-ai-agents-muse-dots-battle). However, the company faced internal turbulence as **The Wall Street Journal reported OpenAI cut ties with three safety researchers** after an internal investigation found they mishandled sensitive information.

### The Infrastructure Race: From Orbital OS to RAM Shortages
The hardware backbone for AI is stretching from Earth to orbit. Google launched its first advanced chip into space to test the waters for **orbital data centers**. A TechCrunch analysis notes that [Google believes SpaceX's Starship needs 1,600 launches](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/google-thinks-spacexs-starship-has-to-launch-1600-times-before-space-data-centers-get-off-the-ground/) to make such a vision economically feasible. Meanwhile, startup **Satlyt raised $8M** to build the "Android of orbital computing," offering open software to run AI on various satellites, contrasting with SpaceX's closed approach.

Back on Earth, a major constraint persists. **Memory executives expect the RAM shortage to continue through 2028**, with Micron's CEO stating prices for 2027 are "much higher than 2026 prices," [Ars Technica reports](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/10/memory-supplies-are-only-getting-tighter-micron-ceo-says/). This tight supply could hamstring AI development and deployment costs. In a cautionary tale about data center hype, The Verge published a deep-dive investigation into [Kevin O'Leary's troubled plans for a massive Utah AI data center](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1002851/utah-ai-data-center-stratos-kevin-oleary-investigation-backlash).

### Startups Bet on an Agent-First Future
A clear theme emerges: startups are preparing for a world where AI agents, not apps, are the primary interface. **Photon, which held a symbolic funeral for mobile apps, raised $4.5M** to help developers build agents that operate over iMessage, SMS, and email. In an interview, **Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky argued that AI agents need their own operating system**, stating the current ecosystem isn't built for them.

This agent-centric approach is spreading to other domains. **Shopify debuted "Canvas,"** an AI site builder where merchants chat with an agent called Sidekick to build stores in real-time. **Flow Engineering raised funding at a $750M valuation** to bring AI agents to hardware design. Even Amazon is joining the trend, with AWS's Strand Labs [releasing "Strands Decider 2B,"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/amazon-releases-its-own-jev-clone-as-decision-models-flood-the-web/) its own version of a popular decision-making model architecture.

### Editorial: The Execution Layer
Today's news highlights a pivotal shift from what AI *can* do to what it *will* do—the execution layer. OpenAI's essay frames AI as the "eternal complement" for human ideas, a theme echoed in Shopify's builder and Albertsons' operations. Simultaneously, the frantic infrastructure build-out (in space and on Earth) and the startup gold rush into agents reveal a collective bet that AI's next chapter is about pervasive, useful action. The catch? This requires immense resources, stable hardware supplies, and, as OpenAI's internal issues remind us, navigating the human complexities behind the code.
