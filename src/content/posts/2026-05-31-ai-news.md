### The Murky Fringes of AI Marketing
The intersection of AI, crypto, and commerce produced some particularly odd and troubling stories today. First, a journalist details being personally targeted by a bizarre ad for **Gudtrip**, an AI-powered vape that supposedly delivers Bitcoin with every hit, in a story that reads like a parody of tech-bro excess [*(I went looking for the AI weed vape that gives you Bitcoin for smoking)*](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/933916/ai-powered-crypto-cannabis-vape). More disturbingly, a report exposes how AI grifters are using generated images of fake Black people in emotional, manipulative videos to sell cheap goods on platforms like TikTok Shop, a modern and automated form of digital blackface [*(AI grifters are creating fake Black people to sell Shein junk)*](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/938844/ai-tiktok-shop-blackface-shein-dropshipping). These cases highlight the darkly creative and unethical ways AI is being deployed in the attention economy.

### The High Cost of AI Power & Tools
The scale and expense of the AI race came into sharp focus elsewhere. **SoftBank** announced a staggering plan to invest up to €75 billion to build data centers in France, aiming to add up to 5 gigawatts of capacity to feed the continent's AI hunger [*(SoftBank says it will invest up to €75 billion to build French data centers)*](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/30/softbank-says-it-will-invest-up-to-e75-billion-to-build-french-data-centers/). Meanwhile, the cost of using AI tools is becoming a pain point for developers, as **GitHub Copilot's** shift to a token-based billing model is causing significant consternation, with many users seeing their costs spike and declaring the "golden age" of the coding assistant over [*(‘What a joke’: Github Copilot’s new token-based billing spurs consternation among devs)*](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/30/what-a-joke-github-copilots-new-token-based-billing-spurs-consternation-among-devs/).

### New Products & A Counter-Movement
On the product front, **Meta** is reportedly developing an AI-powered pendant, continuing its push into dedicated AI hardware [*(Meta is reportedly developing an AI pendant)*](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/30/meta-is-reportably-developing-an-ai-pendant/). **Google** has a new persistent AI assistant called Gemini Spark that a reviewer found surprisingly useful for automating daily tasks, though its reason for being a separate product remains unclear [*(I put Google’s 24/7 AI assistant Gemini Spark to work, and it’s actually pretty useful)*](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/30/i-put-googles-24-7-ai-assistant-gemini-spark-to-work-and-its-actually-pretty-useful/). The AI integration trend is also reshaping the browser wars, with several new contenders betting on built-in AI agents as their key differentiator [*(As the browser wars heat up, here are the hottest alternatives to Chrome and Safari in 2026)*](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/30/as-the-browser-wars-heat-up-here-are-the-hottest-alternatives-to-chrome-and-safari-in-2026/). In a notable counterpoint, one founder is finding success by deliberately avoiding the AI hype cycle, building a simple, popular website about historical maps as a bet on "the old school web" [*(How one founder’s bet on ‘the old school web’ is paying off)*](https://www.theverge.com/tech/938245/past-maps-website-google-zero-ai).

Finally, a sharp commentary warns that the upcoming **SpaceX IPO**, driven heavily by Musk's AI and robotics ambitions for the company, is structured in a way that heavily benefits insiders at the potential expense of public investors [*(The SpaceX IPO is great for Elon Musk and terrible for you)*](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/940001/elon-musk-spacex-ipo-ai).

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of an AI ecosystem in a tense adolescence. The technology is powerful enough to attract €75 billion investments and reshape major software tools, yet it's also being used for cheap grifts and manipulative marketing. The simultaneous rise of a "post-AI" nostalgia movement and debates over who pays for the compute highlight the growing pains as this technology moves from novelty to infrastructure.
