Today’s biggest stories point to AI moving from clever demos into the messy places where identity, money, infrastructure, and public power collide.

## Meta’s Muse becomes a video-call companion

Meta said U.S. users can now [video chat with their Muse AI avatar](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/meta-says-us-users-can-now-video-chat-with-their-muse-ai-avatar/), giving the assistant a more personal, animated presence. The Verge’s hands-on of Muse’s new [voice mode](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1028015/meta-muse-ai-voice-mode-expressive-avatar) describes the feature as a real-time, expressive way to talk with the agent. The update comes as Meta also previews a Tamagotchi-like wearable intended to bring Muse to life.

## AI gadgets, music, and movies keep pushing into daily life

Aina is betting that AI agents need a physical companion. Its smart ring has [a screen, a joystick, and a mic](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/ainas-smart-ring-has-a-screen-a-joystick-and-a-mic-so-you-can-talk-to-ai-agents/) so users can talk to agents, take calls, and dictate notes on a Mac.

In creative tools, SoundCloud is adding integration with ElevenLabs so users can [post AI music straight to SoundCloud](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1027427/ai-music-soundcloud-elevenlabs) without exporting and uploading separately.

Meanwhile, the AI entertainment experiment is getting a bigger star: Grimes will appear in the AI-generated sci-fi film *Gods Don’t Give Gifts*, billed by The Verge as [the first AI film to come to theatres](https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1026282/grimes-ai-movie).

## Claude wants to manage your money

Anthropic is pushing Claude into the sensitive world of personal finance. TechCrunch reports that Claude can now [analyze your spending with its new Personal Finance tool](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/claude-can-now-analyze-your-spending-with-its-new-personal-finance-tool/), connecting bank and card accounts through Plaid. The Verge’s version of the story frames it as a pitch for letting [Claude plug directly into your bank account](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1027652/anthropic-claude-plaid-personal-finance) and answer questions like, “Where did my money go this month?”

## OpenAI and the enterprise AI stack chase credibility

OpenAI released a new cybersecurity model, and TechCrunch asked the obvious follow-up: [is that enough?](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/openai-released-a-new-cybersecurity-model-is-that-enough/) The Verge reports that OpenAI says [GPT-6 Cyber is better at finding vulnerabilities and less likely to go rogue](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1027599/openai-gpt-6-cyber-launch), with out-of-bounds behavior under 1% in testing.

OpenAI is also talking about its own financial future. President Greg Brockman told TechCrunch the company [could be IPO ready in 2027](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/openai-president-greg-brockman-says-company-could-be-ipo-ready-in-2027/), pointing to accelerating enterprise revenue and a business turnaround.

The AI infrastructure layer keeps consolidating. Dropbox acquired Tigris Data for [$350 million to bolster its AI infrastructure](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/dropbox-acquires-tigris-data-for-350m-to-bolster-its-ai-iinfrastructure/), supporting the development of DBX Agent Cloud. Box, meanwhile, is tapping Nvidia’s Nemotron for a new [agent-first content retrieval system](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/box-taps-nvidias-nemotron-for-new-agent-first-content-retrieval-system/) that it says answers queries four times faster, with 20% fewer errors and 90% fewer tokens used.

## Models are getting cheaper, faster, and more verifiable

Google introduced [Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemini-omni-1-2-flash/), positioning it as a fast, cost-effective model for longer clips, while DeepMind highlighted the same update in its [developer-focused post](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-omni-12-flash-fast-and-cost-effective-with-longer-clips/).

In research, Axiom Math is trying to solve a problem AI-generated content has made worse: [keeping AI slop out of mathematics](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/axiom-math-wants-to-keep-ai-slop-out-of-mathematics/). Its AI agents just formally verified the Stacks Project, one of the field’s biggest libraries.

And a YC-backed startup is putting [$1 million behind five unsolved materials science problems](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/a-yc-startup-is-putting-1m-behind-five-unsolved-materials-science-problems-for-ai/) for AI, betting that frontier models can finally crack the complex physics of advanced materials.

## Agility goes public, and humanoid robotics gets a stock-market test

Agility Robotics is now trading on Nasdaq as the [first publicly traded pure-play humanoid robotics company](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/agility-is-the-first-pure-humanoid-robot-company-on-the-stock-market-are-you-buying/). That makes it an early public-market referendum on whether humanoid robots can move from demos to durable business models.

## Politics, backlash, and the physical cost of AI

Meta warned that [election backlash against data centers](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/metas-warns-that-election-backlash-against-data-centers-could-make-ai-expansion-harder/) could make future AI expansion harder as communities push back against massive infrastructure projects.

The political stakes got sharper after Democrats took control of the House and Senate in a wave election that The Verge described as [“total repudiation” that is about to hit AI](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1026927/total-repudiation-the-blue-wave-is-about-to-hit-ai), with historic firsts including Georgia’s first Black woman governor and New Mexico’s first Native American woman governor.

And The Verge’s Decoder podcast examined the [hidden supply chain costs of the AI boom](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1024438/willy-shih-supply-chain-ai-chips-ram-where-does-my-stuff-come-from-book), with Harvard Business School economist Willy Shih explaining how chips, RAM, and global manufacturing bottlenecks are shaping the real-world limits of AI.

## The day’s theme

The common thread is that AI is no longer just a model race. It is becoming a political economy: avatars and rings bring assistants into daily life; Claude and GPT-6 Cyber bring them into finance and security; data centers, supply chains, and elections bring them into public policy. The winners in the next phase will need to prove not only capability, but trust, accountability, and affordability.
