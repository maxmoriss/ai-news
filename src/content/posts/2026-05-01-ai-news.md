### Enterprise AI Gets Serious
The focus on **governance and control** in corporate AI is intensifying. SAP argues that proper enterprise AI governance, replacing statistical guesses with deterministic control, is key to securing profit margins ([source](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/sap-how-enterprise-ai-governance-secures-profit-margins/)). Microsoft is applying a similar principle of structured control with a new **AI agent specifically for legal teams** inside Word, designed to follow legal workflows rather than general commands ([source](https://www.theverge.com/news/921944/microsoft-word-legal-agent-ai)). Meanwhile, the **Pentagon has secured classified AI deals** with major players like OpenAI, Google, and Nvidia, but notably left out Anthropic from this round ([source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/922113/pentagon-ai-classified-openai-google-nvidia)).

### Market Moves & Monetization
The AI market is showing signs of maturation and fierce competition. **GitHub Copilot** is shifting to a per-token charging model from a flat-rate subscription, signaling a move towards more granular, usage-based pricing for developer tools ([source](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/per-token-ai-charging-comes-to-github-copilot/)). In the legal tech arena, startup **Legora** has hit a $5.6B valuation, heating up its battle with rival Harvey ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/legal-ai-startup-legora-hits-5-6-valuation-and-its-battle-with-harvey-just-got-hotter/)). On the funding front, **Anthropic** is reportedly moving swiftly towards a potential valuation round that could exceed $900B ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/anthropic-potential-900b-valuation-round-could-happen-within-two-weeks/)), while Apple admits it was surprised by **AI-driven demand for its Macs** ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/apple-was-surprised-by-ai-driven-demand-for-macs/)). Stripe is facilitating agent economies with **Link**, a digital wallet that autonomous AI agents can also use for spending ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/stripe-link-digital-wallet-ai-agents-shopping/)).

### Security, Scrutiny, and Slop
Security concerns are paramount. OpenAI announced **new advanced security for ChatGPT accounts**, including a partnership with hardware security key provider Yubico ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/openai-announces-new-advanced-security-for-chatgpt-accounts-including-a-partnership-with-yubico/)). Following its criticism of Anthropic for limiting access to its model Mythos, OpenAI itself is **restricting initial access to its cybersecurity testing tool, GPT-5.5 Cyber** ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/after-dissing-anthropic-for-limiting-mythos-openai-restricts-access-to-cyber-too/)). A severe new Linux threat, "CopyFail," is also causing global concern ([source](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/04/as-the-most-severe-linux-threat-in-years-surfaces-the-world-scrambles/)). On the ethics front, a report details how **Christian content creators are outsourcing AI-generated "slop"** to gig workers on platforms like Fiverr ([source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/920881/ai-generated-bible-videos-christian-creators-fiverr-slop)).

### Musk vs. OpenAI: The Trial Continues
The high-profile lawsuit between **Elon Musk and OpenAI** dominated headlines. Musk testified that his company, xAI, **trained its Grok model on OpenAI models**, bringing the concept of "distillation" into the courtroom ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/elon-musk-testifies-that-xai-trained-grok-on-openai-models/)). Legal analysts suggest Musk is unlikely to win his case ([source](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/922009/musk-openai-trial-testimony-vergecast)), and a major procedural error by Musk's team while the jury was out of the room may have compounded their problems ([source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/921713/musk-v-altman-jared-birchall-screw-up-xai)).

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of AI moving from a wild west of innovation into a phase of **consolidation, regulation, and commercial pragmatism**. Enterprises and governments are demanding control and security (SAP, Microsoft, Pentagon), while the market is rationalizing through new pricing models (GitHub Copilot) and sky-high valuations for specialized players (Legora, Anthropic). The courtroom drama between Musk and OpenAI underscores the intense competition and contentious IP battles defining this era. The overarching theme is a shift from what AI *can* do to how it *should* be managed, paid for, and deployed responsibly.
