### 🔌 AI's Energy Crunch Meets Compute Sovereignty
The explosive growth of AI is hitting a physical limit: the power grid. **Data centers may face temporary power cuts** on the largest US grid as operators scramble to keep up with demand from new facilities. This highlights a growing infrastructural challenge as AI scales. Meanwhile, smaller nations are seeking strategic independence in this compute-hungry era. **Armenia is making a calculated bet on compute sovereignty**, aiming to secure its own AI future not through chip manufacturing, but by ensuring access to and control over critical computational resources. [Read about the grid strain on TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/data-centers-may-face-temporary-power-cuts-to-prevent-blackouts-on-largest-us-grid/) | [Read about Armenia's strategy on AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/armenias-ai-bet-is-not-chip-manufacturing-it-is-compute-sovereignty/)

### 💭 Editorial: The Centralization Tension
Today's news paints a picture of AI at a crossroads. On one hand, we see massive centralization: giant compute deals, a few platforms (Google, Microsoft) becoming default AI providers, and warnings from leaders like **Satya Nadella** that companies relying on a single AI may not survive. On the other, there's a push for sovereignty (Armenia), local agency (Perplexity's PC), and open models, all while grappling with the dire consequences of centralized platforms failing to police abuse. The tension between the efficiency of centralized, powerful AI and the resilience and safety of a more distributed ecosystem is becoming the defining struggle of this phase.
