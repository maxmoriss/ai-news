### Nvidia's Growing Crisis
The controversy surrounding Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's alleged relationship with a suspected Chinese spy deepened today. Reports reveal that **a short seller flagged the ties six months before the scandal** became public, questioning the "unusually close" relationship. The fallout is accelerating: **the race is on to sue Nvidia** as its stock slid over 4%, with legal experts suggesting the company's initial dismissal of the issue as "normal" could prove costly. Huang has pulled out of major industry events, including the ElevenLabs Summit. Most significantly, **the affair puts Nvidia's China ambitions under scrutiny**, potentially complicating its lobbying efforts to loosen U.S. chip export restrictions as it seeks to maintain access to a critical market.

### Model & Platform Updates
*   **Anthropic** had a busy day, releasing two new models: **Claude Mythos and Fable 5.2**. The company claims improvements in coding, reasoning, and vision. In a more surprising move, **Anthropic joined the AI natural gas frenzy**, purchasing $3.4 billion worth of turbines to power its data centers—a massive bet on its own energy future.
*   **OpenAI** made **ChatGPT Enterprise available in the AWS Marketplace**, simplifying procurement for businesses using AWS. Separately, the company detailed its **"Control Room,"** a central system to track and stop model workloads across its research infrastructure for safety reasons.
*   **Meta** released **Muse Ember**, a new lightweight and cost-effective model designed for AI agent execution, priced at 10 cents per million input tokens.

### Products & Policy
*   **Apple** has quietly introduced **paid "Intelligence Usage Packs."** After setting usage limits for some AI features in creator apps like Logic Pro, users can now buy more capacity for a one-time fee of $49.99.
*   On the policy front, a **major SSH vulnerability nicknamed "*sshole"** is under active exploitation, posing a serious threat to Linux devices and internet security.
*   **Former DOJ antitrust chief Jonathan Kanter** is now using AI tools in the private sector to continue his fight against Big Tech monopolies.

Nvidia's saga is a stark reminder that geopolitical tensions and executive conduct can swiftly eclipse technical prowess, threatening multi-billion dollar market strategies. Meanwhile, Anthropic's enormous gas turbine purchase highlights the industry's desperate, physical scramble for power—the literal fuel for the AI race. The era of pure software innovation is colliding with hard realities of geopolitics, energy, and law.
