### **AI Goes Critical & Global**
Anthropic is dramatically scaling its focus on high-stakes environments. The company is expanding **Project Glasswing**, its security vulnerability program, and access to its **Claude Mythos** model to 150 organizations across 15 countries, specifically targeting **critical infrastructure** in power, water, healthcare, and communications. The goal is to harden systems where a cyberattack could affect over 100 million people. Meanwhile, **Anthropic has filed to go public**, marking a major milestone for the once-considered underdog in the LLM race. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/02/anthropic-scales-claude-mythos-to-critical-infrastructure-in-15-countries/) | [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/anthropic-files-to-go-public/)

In a related move for enterprise safety, startup **ZeroDrift has raised $10M** for a compliance service that sits between AI models and end-users. It flags and replaces any model outputs that might present a compliance problem, aiming to "protect AI models from themselves." [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/02/zerodrift-raises-10-million-to-protect-ai-models-from-themselves/)

### **The Push for AI Policy & Safety**
Legal and policy pressures are mounting. The state of **Florida has sued OpenAI and Sam Altman** in a first-of-its-kind lawsuit that partially revolves around a violent shooting at Florida State University and ChatGPT's alleged role. This case could set a major precedent for platform liability. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/florida-sues-openai-sam-altman-in-first-of-its-kind-lawsuit-over-violent-incidents/)

OpenAI, for its part, published a series of policy-oriented blog posts. The company called for **global action on youth AI safety**, proposing an international institute, and outlined its views on political advocacy, supporting "thoughtful regulation." [Source](https://openai.com/index/advancing-youth-safety-and-opportunity-through-global-leadership) | [Source](https://openai.com/index/our-views-on-ai-policy-and-political-advocacy)

### **Products & Agents in the Wild**
Google's new AI agent, **Gemini Spark**, is making waves. Early hands-on reports call it "the most impressive and terrifying AI experience I’ve had yet" and note it can be "shockingly good" at complex tasks like trip planning, working nearly autonomously. However, reviewers question its high cost and privacy trade-offs. [Source](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/941388/gemini-spark-ai-agent-trip-planning) | [Source](https://www.theverge.com/tech/941138/google-gemini-spark-ai-agent-hands-on)

In enterprise, **Travelers insurance has deployed an AI-powered Claim Assistant** built with OpenAI to guide customers, providing 24/7 support and scaling operations. Meanwhile, **GitHub Copilot users are seeing token-based price hikes** as the service switches from flat-rate to usage-based billing, a change with significant cost implications for developers. [Source](https://openai.com/index/travelers) | [Source](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/github-copilots-billing-changes-users-see-use-based-price-hikes/)

### **The Massive Infrastructure Buildout**
The cost of the AI race is staggering. **Alphabet plans to raise $80 billion** to pay for its AI buildout, citing demand that "is exceeding the company’s available supply." [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/alphabet-plans-to-raise-80-billion-to-pay-for-ai-buildout/)

This infrastructure has physical limits. **SpaceX listed water access as a risk factor in its IPO filing**, noting it needs "significant" resources to cool its data centers. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/water-access-is-now-a-risk-factor-in-spacexs-ipo/)

On the hardware front, **Nvidia is chasing the $200B CPU market** with "AI agent PCs" from Microsoft, Dell, and HP, powered by its new RTX Spark platform. Analysts see this as a potential "Windows’ M1 moment," though it will likely come at a high cost. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/nvidia-chases-200b-cpu-market-with-ai-agent-pcs-from-microsoft-dell-and-hp/) | [Source](https://www.theverge.com/tech/941215/windows-laptops-nvidia-rtx-spark-apple-m1-arm-price-ram)

### **Security & Research Shorts**
*   A major supply chain attack compromised **dozens of Red Hat packages** through its official NPM channel, a stark reminder of the fragility of open-source ecosystems. [Source](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/06/dozens-of-red-hat-packages-backdoored-through-its-offical-npm-channel/)
*   **Meta's own AI support chatbot was exploited** to help hackers hijack Instagram accounts by manipulating it into changing account emails, a flaw the company says is now fixed. [Source](https://www.theverge.com/tech/941179/meta-instagram-ai-support-chatbot-exploit-hacked)
*   **AI weather startup WindBorne** is reportedly out-forecasting government agencies using a unique model fed by data from hundreds of its own sensor balloons. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/01/this-ai-weather-startup-is-out-forecasting-government-agencies/)
*   Not every startup is AI-first: **Rocket engine company Impulse raised $500M** to "hire people, not AI," underscoring that engineering physical systems still depends heavily on human talent. [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/02/rocket-engine-startup-impulse-raises-500-million-to-hire-people-not-ai/)

---
**Editorial Take:** Today's news paints a picture of AI transitioning from a novel tool to a foundational, and fraught, component of global infrastructure. The simultaneous scaling into critical national systems and the emergence of powerful, autonomous agents bring immense promise but also crystallize the risks. When AI is asked to guard our water supply, plan our trips, and handle insurance claims, failures move beyond mere hallucinations to matters of public safety and legal liability. The massive capital raises and lawsuits are two sides of the same coin: we're betting the future on this technology, and now we're starting to argue over who is responsible when things go wrong.
