### The Battle for Content & Trust
The value of high-quality, current data is skyrocketing, and tech giants are opening their wallets. **Apple is reportedly in talks** with major publishers, considering a nine-figure budget to license news content for Siri, aiming to power more current and authoritative answers. Meanwhile, **Amazon has clarified its policy** for Twitch streamers: their content will be used to train Amazon's AI models by default, with an opt-out now available. A Twitch executive bluntly stated that if it were opt-in, "nobody would opt in."

This push for data is colliding with user trust and security. A **massive supply-chain attack** has leaked terabytes of credentials, scraped from 2,500 users of a compromised AI package. On a different front, **Anthropic is facing user backlash** over new watermarking for its Claude AI, with some complaining it will catch them using the tool for work or school tasks without permission.

### Enterprise AI Heats Up (and Consolidates)
The race to bring AI to businesses is accelerating with eye-popping valuations. **Microsoft is unifying its Copilot apps** into a single "super app" interface, merging consumer and commercial assistants. In funding news, the AI coding startup **Cognition is already in talks** for a new round that could value it at a staggering $40 billion, just months after a $26 billion valuation. Furthermore, **OpenAI-backed Thrive Holdings** has raised $2 billion at a $12 billion valuation to bring AI to the enterprise.

Other companies are focusing on efficiency and specialization. Identity management firm **Okta announced a new feature** targeting the "tool tax"—the token cost overhead when AI agents consider which tools to use—promising significant cost savings. **Google DeepMind published research** on a breakthrough sign-language-to-text model, aiming to put translation AI directly into users' hands.

### Hardware, Health, and Existential Questions
AI is pushing into new physical and clinical domains. At the Made by Google '26 event, the tech giant unveiled a slew of **new Pixel devices packed with Gemini AI features**. Wearable startup **Sandbar is betting on voice** as the future interface for AI hardware, specifically with a smart ring for note-taking. In healthcare, **Google is testing its AMIE AI system** for clinical video consultations, with early studies showing it performed on par with primary care physicians in simulated scenarios.

Amidst this breakneck pace, foundational debates continue. In a notable discussion at the Ai4 conference, **AI pioneers Geoffrey Hinton, Fei-Fei Li, and Andrew Ng made the case for maintaining open access** to AI research, even as safety concerns mount. This stands in contrast to the internal scrutiny suggested by a Verge podcast asking: **"Does Google even want to win at AI?"** following a major reorganization of its Google DeepMind division.

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of an industry at an inflection point, where the cost of progress isn't just measured in dollars (of which there are billions flowing), but in ethical compromises, security risks, and a fundamental tension between open innovation and controlled deployment. The move from "opt-in" to "opt-out" for data usage, alongside tools to detect AI use, signals a new phase where user agency is often an afterthought to the hunger for training data and model improvement. The path forward will be defined by who manages these trade-offs most responsibly.
