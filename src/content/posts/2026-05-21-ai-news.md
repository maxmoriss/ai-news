### **The Compute Arms Race Intensifies**
The true cost of the AI boom is coming into sharp, staggering focus. **Anthropic** is set to pay **Elon Musk's xAI a staggering $1.25 billion *per month*** for access to compute capacity in SpaceX's Colossus data centers, a deal totaling **$15 billion annually** [according to a SpaceX IPO filing](https://www.theverge.com/science/935229/spacex-anthropic-ipo-ai-capacity-deal-colossus). This colossal expense isn't stopping Anthropic's march; the company has told investors it's on track for **its first profitable quarter**, projecting Q2 revenue to more than double to ~$10.9 billion [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/anthropic-says-its-about-to-have-its-first-profitable-quarter/). Meanwhile, xAI itself **burned through $6.4 billion last year** and is now planning to buy **$2.8 billion more in natural gas turbines** for its data centers, even as it faces a lawsuit over its existing generators [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/xai-burned-6-4b-last-year-spacexs-ipo-filing-shows-why-the-spending-is-far-from-over/).

On the hardware front, **Nvidia's** dominance continues, but CEO Jensen Huang is pointing to a new frontier: **AI agent CPUs**. He predicts a **"brand new" $200 billion market** for processors that power autonomous AI agents, a bet embodied in the company's often-overlooked Vera chip [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/jensen-huang-says-hes-found-a-brand-new-200b-market-for-nvidia/). The chip giant also revealed it holds a **$43 billion portfolio of startup investments** alongside another record earnings quarter [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/nvidia-posts-another-record-quarter-reveals-43-billion-of-holdings-in-startups/).

### **Big Bets & Big Layoffs**
The pressure to fund AI ambitions is reshaping corporate strategies. **Meta** is laying off "thousands of employees" in an effort to offset its massive AI investments and run the company "more efficiently" [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/935163/meta-layoffs-ai-investment-offset-memo). In contrast, a new secretive startup, **Hark**, founded by Brett Adcock, has raised a jaw-dropping **$700 million Series A at a $6 billion valuation** for its work on a "universal" AI interface [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/21/hark-raises-700m-series-a-for-its-secretive-universal-ai-interface/).

Government is also placing big bets. The **U.S. government is taking a $2 billion equity stake in nine quantum computing firms**, a move underscoring the national strategic importance of next-generation computing, with beneficiaries including a startup backed by a firm with links to the Trump family [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/05/us-government-takes-2-billion-equity-stake-in-nine-quantum-computing-firms/).

### **Products & Policy: From Therapy to Agents**
In the product sphere, the push for practical and safe AI is accelerating. **The Path**, a new startup founded by Tony Robbins and Calm alums, is launching with claims its AI therapy model scored a 95 on the Vera-MH safety benchmark, far above the 65 scored by leading consumer bots [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/21/the-path-founded-by-tony-robbins-and-calm-alums-wants-to-offer-safer-ai-therapy/). **Google** is making a major consumer push for its **AI agent ecosystem**, though the article questions whether users are ready for a proliferation of autonomous digital assistants [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/21/google-is-pitching-an-ai-agent-ecosystem-to-consumers-who-may-not-buy-it/). On the developer side, Google's "vibe coding" in Gemini AI Studio allowed a Verge reporter to build and deploy multiple Android apps in a single afternoon with minimal code [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/935056/google-vibe-coding-first-android-app-gemini-ai-studio).

Other startups are applying AI to industrial problems: companies are using **AI-powered sorting to capitalize on a 20% spike in aluminum prices** by improving recycling yields [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/21/with-aluminum-prices-up-20-recycling-startups-bet-on-ai-to-cash-in/), while **Clouted** raised $7 million to use AI to take the "guesswork out of making short videos go viral" [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/clouted-wants-to-take-the-guesswork-out-of-making-short-videos-go-viral/).

**Editorial Take:** Today's news paints a picture of an industry at an inflection point. The astronomical figures—$15B in annual compute costs, $700M Series A rounds—highlight a market transitioning from pure, subsidized research to a brutal fight for sustainable profitability. The simultaneous layoffs at Meta and massive funding for Hark show capital being ruthlessly reallocated toward perceived future winners. The theme is a costly pivot to product: whether it's Anthropic chasing profit, Nvidia hunting the next $200B market, or startups building therapy bots and recycling tech, the era of selling the dream is giving way to the hard work of building viable businesses.
