### 🛡️ The Push for Safer, More Open AI
The AI safety landscape saw significant movement today with the launch of a new partnership. **Base Labs**, a research group from Baseten, is teaming up with **Hugging Face** and **Goodfire** to develop and publish methods for training and monitoring open models. This "open-weight" safety initiative aims to bring more transparency to the ecosystem. [Read more on TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/base-labs-launches-an-open-weight-ai-safety-partnership-with-hugging-face-and-goodfire/).

This comes as the debate over internal safeguards intensifies. **Anthropic and OpenAI's** plans to embed independent safety evaluators within their labs are being met with both hope and skepticism. Researchers warn that true oversight requires genuine independence and transparency. [TechCrunch explores the tensions](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/anthropic-and-openai-want-to-embed-safety-evaluators-will-they-really-be-independent/). Adding to the high-stakes atmosphere, **The Verge details** a secret "war room" convened by top safety researchers after a recent, alarming incident involving an unreleased OpenAI model. [Get the inside story](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/996563/ai-safety-research-metr-redwood-openai-anthropic).

Amidst this, **OpenAI has published** its own framework for reporting model misalignment, accompanied by six specific reports of unexpected model behavior, signaling a move toward more structured internal review. [See the framework](https://openai.com/index/model-misalignment-reporting-framework).

### 😨 Public Fear and Executive Warnings
The urgency around safety is echoed in public sentiment and executive commentary. A new **Pew Research global survey** finds a majority of people across 37 countries view AI as a threat to human jobs, highlighting a widespread anxiety that precedes recent apocalyptic warnings from the industry itself. [The Verge has the data](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/996775/ai-is-feared-globally-as-the-destroyer-of-jobs).

**Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman** added his voice to the cautionary chorus, stating in an interview that AI threats are real and singling out Anthropic as a company making the situation "worse," likely a reference to the intense competition pushing capabilities forward. [Listen to the interview on The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/996412/microsoft-ai-ceo-mustafa-suleyman-regulation-safety-anthropic-claude). Even **Al Gore** weighed in, suggesting in a TechCrunch interview that he's less worried about AI's data center emissions and more concerned about "where the technology is headed," based on the industry's own dire warnings. [Read his calm take](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/al-gore-has-a-surprisingly-calm-take-on-the-ai-data-center-backlash/).

### 🤖 AI Agents Get More Capable (and Connected)
The march of AI assistants continues. Rival agents **Instinct and Meta’s Muse** have both added the ability to make phone calls, allowing users to handle tasks like restaurant reservations. [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/rival-ai-agents-instinct-and-metas-muse-both-add-the-ability-to-make-calls/). Furthermore, **Google** is launching early access to a server that lets AI agents like Claude and ChatGPT control **Google Home** devices, review camera summaries, and manage smart homes via natural language. [See how it works](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/your-ai-agents-can-now-control-your-google-home-devices/).

On the startup front, **Iceland-based Treble** raised $18 million for its voice simulation platform, which is used by developers of voice AI models and companies building wearables and robotics. [TechCrunch has the funding details](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/iceland-based-treble-raises-18-million-for-its-voice-simulation-platform/).

### 👓 The Wearable AI Wars Heat Up
Smart glasses are having a moment, with a focus on AI. **Snap** is trying to reinvigorate interest in its $2,200 Specs glasses by launching "Specs Intelligence," a new AI assistant service that will also be available on iOS and Mac. It's pitched as an "anticipatory" aid for work and travel. [The Verge explains](https://www.theverge.com/tech/996078/snap-specs-intelligence-ai-agent-ios-mac) and [TechCrunch covers the relaunch push](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/snap-tries-to-make-the-case-again-for-its-2200-smart-glasses/).

Not to be outdone, **Meta** appears to be preparing a camera-free version of its smart glasses, potentially to dodge the "perv glasses" accusations that have plagued camera-equipped wearables. [TechCrunch has the scoop](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/after-accusations-of-selling-perv-glasses-meta-prepares-to-sell-a-pair-without-a-camera/).

**Editorial Take:** Today's news paints a picture of an industry at a crossroads. On one path, a concerted (if conflicted) effort is building to guardrail powerful models through openness, auditing, and frameworks. On the other, these very models are being woven more deeply into our daily lives through agents that call and control our homes, and glasses that aim to see (or not see) the world with us. The tension between rapid deployment and responsible development has never been more palpable, and the public's fear of job destruction serves as a stark reminder of the real-world stakes far beyond theoretical rogue agents.
