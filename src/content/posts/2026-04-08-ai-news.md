### **AI's Cybersecurity Arms Race Intensifies**
The day brought starkly contrasting news on AI and security. On the offensive front, **Anthropic debuted a preview of a powerful new AI model, Mythos**, as part of a high-profile cybersecurity initiative ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/anthropic-mythos-ai-model-preview-security/)). The company also detailed **Project Glasswing**, a new model that reportedly found security problems "in every major operating system and web browser" as part of a partnership with tech giants like Nvidia, Google, and Apple ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/908114/anthropic-project-glasswing-cybersecurity)). On the defensive side, **Microsoft released an open-source toolkit designed to secure AI agents at runtime**, aiming to impose strict governance on autonomous models executing code on corporate networks ([AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/microsoft-open-source-toolkit-secures-ai-agents-at-runtime/)). Meanwhile, a sobering reminder of real-world threats emerged: **Thousands of end-of-life consumer routers in 120 countries have been hacked by Russia's military** to steal credentials ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/04/russias-military-hacks-thousands-of-consumer-routers-to-steal-credentials/)).

### **Enterprise AI Adoption Hits Production, But Faces Growing Pains**
A survey from OutSystems, *The State of AI Development 2026*, indicates that **AI has moved into an early production phase for many enterprises**, primarily within IT functions ([AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/ai-workflows-developer-success-and-central-project-management-needs/)). However, the survey of 1,879 IT leaders warns that adoption is running ahead of central project management needs, creating potential risks. In the physical world, a partnership between **Thrive Logic and Asylon aims to bring "physical AI" to enterprise perimeter security**, combining autonomous robotic patrols with AI analytics ([AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/physical-ai-security-at-the-enterprise-perimeter-takes-a-step-closer/)). Atlassian is bringing AI deeper into workflows, **launching visual AI tools and third-party agents in Confluence** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/08/atlassian-confluence-visual-ai-tools-agents/)). And **Uber is expanding its AWS contract to run more features on Amazon's AI chips**, a notable win for Amazon's silicon ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/uber-is-the-latest-to-be-won-over-by-amazons-ai-chips/)).

The infrastructure feeding the AI boom continues to scale. **Anthropic has bulked up its compute deal with Google and Broadcom** as its run-rate revenue surges to $30 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/anthropic-compute-deal-google-broadcom-tpus/)). **Nvidia-backed AI data center builder Firmus has hit a $5.5B valuation** after raising $1.35 billion in six months ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/firmus-the-southgate-ai-datacenter-builder-backed-by-nvidia-hits-5-5b-valuation/)). On the hardware frontier, **Intel has signed on to Elon Musk's "Terafab" chips project** to build a new semiconductor factory in Texas ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/intel-signs-on-to-elon-musks-terafab-chips-project/)). In the open-source arena, a TechCrunch columnist **expressed rooting for tiny open-source AI model maker Arcee**, a 26-person startup gaining popularity ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/07/i-cant-help-rooting-for-tiny-open-source-ai-model-maker-arcee/)). Labor tensions surfaced as **unionized staff at ProPublica went on a 24-hour strike**, citing concerns over AI, layoffs, and wages ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/908401/propublica-union-strike-negotiations-ai-layoffs)).

### **Editorial Take: The Security Paradox**
Today's headlines paint a picture of AI as both a formidable shield and a potential catalyst for vulnerability. Anthropic and Microsoft are building sophisticated tools to defend digital systems, even as outdated hardware creates immense real-world attack surfaces. The industry's focus is rightly shifting from mere capability to **governance, safety, and runtime security** for increasingly autonomous agents. However, the massive scale of compute deals and data center construction underscores that the raw power driving these advances is itself a potential point of concentration and risk. The path forward requires not just smarter AI, but smarter *and more secure* foundational infrastructure, from chips to routers.
