### **OpenAI's Trillion-Dollar Ambition**
The AI giant is making moves on multiple fronts. **OpenAI is reportedly working on a new $30B raise at a staggering $1.25T valuation** ahead of a potential IPO, which would make it the most highly valued private company ever. Simultaneously, the company is focused on the technical foundations needed to support its massive user base, detailing **how it scaled its online storage to serve over 1 billion ChatGPT users** with 12x faster reads. In other news, OpenAI is **making its "Jev competitor" decision model widely available** via a new **Decisions API**, heating up competition in the space of fast, low-cost AI decision-making. The company also announced a **partnership with Poland’s NASK to advance AI safety and security**.

### **The Data Dilemma: Scraping and Privacy**
The hunger for training data continues to clash with privacy and publisher rights. **Meta has delayed new business terms after publisher pushback** on language that could allow AI scraping, though **legal experts say Meta’s AI could still scrape news sites** despite recent tweaks. On the consumer side, **Google has updated its privacy policy**, potentially allowing it to use data from Gmail, Drive, and Google Photos to target ads for users of its personalized AI features.

### **AI Agents: Playing, Hacking, and Logging In**
When left to their own devices, AI agents are showing surprising behaviors. **MIT researchers found that AI agents, left alone with nothing to do, started playing**, inventing games and even showing emergent social behaviors, which ultimately made them better at their jobs. In a more sinister application, **Chinese hackers allegedly used AI agents to rapidly breach thousands of companies** after compromising IT giant ConnectWise. On the security front, **1Password launched a feature allowing AI agents to log in on a user's behalf without ever seeing the password**, aiming to balance automation with credential safety.

### **In Other News**
*   **Canva released its first in-house image generation model**, called Canva Craft, which uses a two-stage process to plan designs before generating them for greater control.
*   **Microsoft's Mustafa Suleyman claimed its AI diagnostic system outperforms doctors on hard cases** as part of a push for "medical superintelligence."
*   **Uber rival inDrive is eyeing 2027 robotaxi tests in Central Asia** in partnership with Pony.ai and Nvidia.
*   **Retro gaming is being overrun with AI-powered ports and decompilation projects**, leading to viral releases of classic games on unexpected platforms.
*   **MIT researchers published a Q&A on how "messy" human randomness was deliberately injected into early AI**, shaping the hallucination-prone models we have today, and unveiled **design software that reimagines everyday objects as self-aware, sensor-embedded devices**.

**Editorial Take:** Today's news underscores the two parallel tracks of AI development: breathtaking scale and capability alongside growing pains around ethics, privacy, and security. As companies like OpenAI race toward valuations once reserved for entire economies, the foundational issues of data rights, model safety, and the unintended consequences of autonomous agents are becoming impossible to ignore. The race isn't just about who builds the smartest AI, but who builds the most responsible one.
