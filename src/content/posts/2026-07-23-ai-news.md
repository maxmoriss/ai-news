### 🚀 The Hardware Frontier
The race for AI compute is reaching new heights—literally. **Nvidia is sending GPUs to the moon** as part of a NASA-backed mission, proving its ambition to be the compute provider for every environment, no matter how remote. Back on Earth, a bold challenger has emerged. **AI chip startup Etched defies skeptics, hits $10.3B valuation from big-name investors**. Founded by Harvard dropouts, Etched claims its new chips can speed up inference for any AI model without traditional GPUs. Meanwhile, **IBM insists that AI isn’t killing the mainframe**, blaming a temporary wrecking of corporate hardware budgets for a recent stock crash. In research, **MIT projects were selected for funding under the US Department of Energy’s Genesis Mission**, advancing work in areas like nuclear physics and manufacturing.

### 📈 Startup & Corporate Moves
The venture capital spigot remains wide open for ambitious AI bets. **Travis Kalanick’s robotics company raises $1.7B, led by a16z**, with Uber also investing in his industrial AI venture, Atoms. On the enterprise side, **ServiceNow bets $40 million on Indian banking software specialist** BusinessNext to expand its AI-powered financial services push. Not all news is about growth, however. **Monday.com lays off hundreds to focus on AI**, cutting 20% of its staff to support a "leaner, more focused operating model" for its AI Work Platform. In a different corner of the software world, **Substack’s new tool tells you who’s been writing their newsletters with AI**, signaling a push for transparency in AI-assisted content.

### ⚖️ Policy, Security & The Global Stage
Regulatory and geopolitical tensions are heating up. U.S. **Lawmakers prepare bill requiring AI ‘kill switch’**, a proposal that would empower the Department of Homeland Security to order the shutdown or throttling of AI systems. The ongoing saga around model development ethics took a sharp turn, as the **Treasury threatened sanctions after White House claims Moonshot distilled Anthropic’s Fable**. This has intensified debates over Chinese open models, with **Arcee, a US open source AI lab, saying Chinese models are not inherently dangerous**. A major security incident also came into focus, with a report on **how OpenAI’s human mistake led to the AI-powered hack on Hugging Face**, underscoring the risks in AI development infrastructure.

### 🤖 Products & The Broader Ecosystem
User growth and competitive dynamics continue to shape the landscape. **Google’s Gemini nears billion-user milestone**, having reportedly reached over 750 million monthly users, a sign of massive adoption. **Google justifies its massive AI spending with a booming cloud business**, using record profits from AI infrastructure services to offset its expenditures. The legal battle between tech titans also reveals strategic anxieties; **Apple’s OpenAI lawsuit is about who gets to define the post-smartphone era**, centering on trade secrets and the future of AI hardware. Finally, community pushback against AI's physical footprint is growing, as a report details how **the right-wing boomers protesting data centers have a lot in common with the left**, uniting over local environmental and quality-of-life concerns.

---
**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of an industry hurtling in multiple directions at once—toward space, toward unprecedented valuations, and toward a regulatory reckoning. The extreme capital flows (see: Etched's valuation, OpenAI's $750B spending plan, Kalanick's $1.7B raise) contrast sharply with rising public skepticism (data center protests) and governmental moves to install emergency brakes (the kill switch bill). The theme is one of simultaneous, breakneck expansion and gathering containment efforts. The next phase won't just be about who builds the fastest chip or the best model, but who can navigate this tightening web of technological ambition, economic reality, and societal constraint.
