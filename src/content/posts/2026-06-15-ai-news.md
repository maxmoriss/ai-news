### Policy & Protests: The Fallout from an AI Export Ban
A group of dozens of prominent cybersecurity veterans is urging the White House to reverse its recent export control restrictions on Anthropic’s most powerful models, **Fable** and **Mythos**. They argue the ban will hamper defenders' ability to secure critical software, calling it a "dangerous" move that cedes an advantage to malicious actors. The White House's decision, [according to a new report](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/949644/china-white-house-anthropic-mythos), was partly driven by intelligence suggesting a group linked to **China may have accessed Mythos**, raising serious national security concerns. The clash highlights the tension between safeguarding cutting-edge AI and ensuring its defensive benefits are widely available.

### Big Business: Acquisitions, Investments, and IPOs
The AI dealmaking engine is running hot. **Salesforce announced** it is acquiring AI customer service platform **Fin for a staggering $3.6 billion**, planning to integrate its tech to supercharge Salesforce's own AI agent builder, Agentforce. In funding news, **Sarvam AI has become India's newest AI unicorn** after a $234 million funding round led by a $150 million investment from IT services giant **HCLTech**. On the infrastructure side, **Google revealed a $1.5 billion investment** to expand its data center campus in Alabama over 2026 and 2027. And as the IPO race heats up, a [new analysis](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/14/as-ai-companies-race-to-go-public-who-else-is-along-for-the-ride/) looks at the startups trying to "ride that SpaceX IPO wave," while **OpenAI launched a Partner Network** with a $150 million fund to help global partners deploy its technology.

### The New Workforce: Managing AI Agents and Human Displacement
As AI agents become integral "employees," new challenges emerge. Startup **NewCore has emerged with $66 million in funding** to tackle the next frontier of enterprise security: managing the identities and access of AI agents, not just people. This shift coincides with growing labor tensions. **The AI layoff wave is becoming a "powder keg,"** [with a report noting](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/15/the-ai-layoff-wave-is-becoming-a-powder-keg/) the combustible mix of mass tech layoffs and a small cohort of AI insiders accumulating unprecedented wealth.

### Products in Action: From Drones to Satellites
Autonomy is advancing beyond chatbots. **For the first time ever, an Earth observation satellite autonomously found a target** on its own in April, marking a leap in on-board AI processing for space. Closer to Earth, **Skydio CEO Adam Bry discussed** the ethics of autonomous drones in [a Verge podcast](https://www.theverge.com/podcast/949195/skydio-ceo-adam-bry-autonmous-drones-china-red-lines-military), arguing that Silicon Valley shouldn't unilaterally "draw red lines" for their use, especially as competition with China intensifies.

---
**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of an industry hitting inflection points on multiple fronts. The backlash to the Anthropic ban underscores a core dilemma: the very AI tools with immense offensive potential are also our best hope for cyber defense. This policy struggle is happening alongside a dramatic commercial consolidation, where giants like Salesforce are spending billions to own the AI stack, and a new class of startups is being born to manage the AI workforce we're creating. The juxtaposition of soaring valuations with widespread layoffs and ethical debates over autonomous systems suggests the AI revolution's disruptive force is only just beginning to be felt across the economy and society.
