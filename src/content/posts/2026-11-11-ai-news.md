### The Data Center Bottleneck Intensifies
The AI boom's insatiable appetite for compute is colliding with physical reality. Multiple reports detail how the data center construction frenzy is hitting massive roadblocks. Widespread local opposition, grueling permitting delays, and a critical shortage of electrical power are throttling growth. As [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/video/why-the-data-center-you-dread-might-never-even-be-built/), a fundamental issue is that many announced projects may never break ground. This scarcity is forcing cloud providers to get creative with financing: GPU cloud giant **CoreWeave** is now asking customers for [prepayments and borrowing against specific contracts](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/coreweave-turns-to-customer-prepayments-shorter-contracts-to-fund-ai-growth/) to fund its expansion, signaling a tightening capital environment.

### Leadership & Legislative Headwinds
Amid this infrastructure crunch, **Meta** is losing a key architect. Santosh Janardhan, the company's longtime head of infrastructure, [is stepping down](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/meta-is-losing-its-longtime-head-of-infrastructure/). His departure, after 17 years, marks the end of an era as Meta continues its massive AI and metaverse compute build-out.
Meanwhile, **Nvidia** CEO Jensen Huang faces mounting pressure on a different front. House Republicans have called him for a [closed-door briefing](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/congress-to-call-jensen-huang-in-to-answer-for-alleged-chinese-spy-affair/) regarding his alleged affair with a Chinese spy, with governance experts noting the unprecedented nature of the corporate crisis. A related commentary asks if [such behavior is more commonplace](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/is-jensen-huangs-bad-behavior-more-commonplace-than-it-seems/) than publicly known in the tech industry's upper echelons.

### Funding the Future of Chips
Not all hardware news is about constraints. UK-based **Fractile** has secured a massive [$720 million funding round](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/with-720m-round-britains-fractile-gets-a-leg-up-in-the-race-for-ai-chips/), vaulting it into the top tier of well-funded startups racing to develop specialized AI chips as alternatives to Nvidia's dominance.

### AI Product & Research Updates
*   **OpenAI's Scale**: The company's quarterly revenue has [reportedly crossed $10 billion](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/openais-quarterly-revenue-reportedly-crosses-10b-for-the-first-time/) for the first time, a nearly 50% jump.
*   **Google's Tools**: Google announced the [Gemini Decisions API](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/) for fast, calibrated multimodal decisions and launched a waitlist for **Tabletop**, an [AI experiment](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/tabletop/) that generates customizable collaborative spaces for groups.
*   **DeepMind's Science**: Google DeepMind unveiled [AlphaGenome 2](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/), an AI model designed to predict how DNA variations impact specific cell types and disease.
*   **Adobe's Experiments**: At its MAX conference, Adobe showcased wild AI prototypes that can [turn photos into explorable 3D scenes and design characters through conversation](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1031505/adobe-max-2026-sneaks-experiments-ai).

### Agents Get Real
The vision of autonomous AI agents is inching toward practicality. **Databricks** CEO Ali Ghodsi uses an internal AI agent, "Genie," to get the [unvarnished truth](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/databricks-new-ai-agent-tells-ceo-ali-ghodsi-the-unvarnished-truth/) about what's going wrong across the 12,000-person company. Meanwhile, startup **Plurai** aims to [improve AI agent reliability](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/plurai-wants-to-help-ai-agents-work-better-using-the-same-approach-that-helped-get-self-driving-cars-to-roads/) using techniques borrowed from the development of self-driving cars.

**Editorial Take:** Today's stories paint a picture of an AI industry hitting adolescence. The initial euphoria of model capabilities is giving way to the hard realities of infrastructure, governance, and sustainable economics. The scramble for power (both electrical and political) and the creative financing for compute highlight that the next phase of AI advancement will be won not just by algorithms, but by logistics, capital, and operational grit.
